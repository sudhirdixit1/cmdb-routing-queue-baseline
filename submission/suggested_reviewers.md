# Suggested reviewers

Five names, drawn from the line of work the retargeted manuscript's claims
are most exposed to: **forecast evaluation** — tests of predictive ability
for nested and for families of specifications, and the scoring and
evaluation of probability forecasts. The *International Journal of
Forecasting* asks for four to six referees outside the author's own
institution who are not collaborators; the author has no affiliation, no
co-authors and no institutional relationship with any of these researchers.
Four are cited in the manuscript's related-work section, which is disclosed
in each entry. Each entry says what that reviewer is best placed to break,
because a suggested reviewer who cannot damage the paper is not worth
suggesting.

Every e-mail address below was taken on 2026-09-12 from the researcher's own
institutional page; the page is named in each entry. None was guessed.

---

### 1. Michael W. McCracken — Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, United States
*Research Division. Senior Economic Policy Advisor.*
E-mail: Michael.W.McCracken@stls.frb.org (institutional profile,
stlouisfed.org/research/economists/mccracken).
Co-author of the nested-model forecast-accuracy tests (Clark and McCracken,
2001) that ask the single-specification version of this paper's question.
**Best placed to attack:** whether the increment between two nested
pipelines, re-fitted inside a moving-block bootstrap, is the object those
tests would recognise; whether the pointwise interval's undercoverage under
$K/n$ (Section 9) is a nested-model artefact the literature already names;
and whether the paper's refusal to apply the coverage calibration is the
right call.
*Cited in the manuscript.*

### 2. Raffaella Giacomini — University College London, United Kingdom
*Department of Economics. Professor of Economics.*
E-mail: r.giacomini@ucl.ac.uk (institutional profile,
profiles.ucl.ac.uk/7172-raffaella-giacomini).
Author of the conditional predictive-ability test (Giacomini and White,
2006). **Best placed to attack:** whether the forecast origin, which the
paper makes an argument of the estimand, is what a conditional test
conditions on; whether a decomposition of a surface's variance across
analyst choices is a well-posed inferential object; and whether the case
study's three origins are three forecasting problems rather than one.
*Cited in the manuscript.*

### 3. Peter Reinhard Hansen — University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, United States
*Department of Economics. Henry A. Latané Distinguished Professor.*
E-mail: hansen@unc.edu (institutional page, econ.unc.edu/people/hansen-peter).
Author of the test for superior predictive ability and co-author of the
model confidence set, the two family-level procedures the paper places
itself beside. **Best placed to attack:** whether the simultaneous max-*t*
band over a declared family adds anything the model confidence set does not
already return; whether the family-wise coverage measured against a known
answer (Section 9.2) is the right experiment; and whether a resolution
region is a confidence set by another name.
*Cited in the manuscript.*

### 4. Johanna Ziegel — ETH Zurich, Switzerland
*Seminar for Statistics. Professor of Statistics.*
E-mail: ziegel@stat.math.ethz.ch (personal institutional page,
people.math.ethz.ch/~ziegelj, which writes it as "ziegel [at]
stat.math.ethz.ch").
Works on the evaluation of probability forecasts, scoring rules, forecast
dominance and calibration. **Best placed to attack:** whether the area under
the ROC curve is a defensible primary instrument for a probability forecast
of a binary event when proper scores exist; whether the calibration step in
Section 8 (a calibrator fitted inside every refit, and a registered rule
excluding models outside a slope window) is sound; and whether the
decomposition inherits the score's scale in a way that makes its shares
score-dependent.
*Not cited in the manuscript.*

### 5. Tilmann Gneiting — Heidelberg Institute for Theoretical Studies and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, Germany
*Group Leader, Computational Statistics (HITS); Professor of Computational
Statistics (KIT).*
E-mail: tilmann.gneiting@h-its.org (institutional page,
h-its.org/people/prof-dr-tilmann-gneiting).
Co-author of the strictly proper scoring rules paper the manuscript cites.
**Best placed to attack:** the choice of instruments; the claim that the
paper's objects apply to any increment in a score between two nested
pipelines; and whether the net-benefit reading of Section 8 is a proper
decision-based evaluation or an improper one at a fixed threshold.
*Cited in the manuscript.*
