
\section{Multi-log results}
\label{sec:multilog}

\label{sec:denominator}
This section reports the surface on every admitted pair: what each analyst
choice is worth, where a sign is determined, and what the conventional
one-number report costs. All point estimates come from one pass of the frozen
pipeline over the \nPairs\ log--target pairs; intervals are the basic
construction of Section~\ref{sec:boot} and bands the whole-surface
construction of Section~\ref{sec:simbands}.

\subsection{The master table}

\input{tables/master}

Table~\ref{tab:master} has one row per log--target pair: the increment at the
reference cell --- the conventional report of Section~\ref{sec:surface} ---
with its simultaneous band, the counts of beneficial, harmful and unresolved
cells, the region label, the robustness index and the sign-disagreement rate.
The reference cell's sign and the region label need not agree, and on
several pairs they do not: a surface can be conditionally beneficial while
the one cell an analyst would have stood on is negative, because the label
ranges over the whole inference family and the reference cell is one member
of it.

Each entry of Table~\ref{tab:sobol} is a median over the \nInstruments\
metrics, computed within each metric in its own units. The pooled surface's
largest first-order index at the median pair, \largestFirstOrderPct, is a
different quantity --- the median over pairs of the median over metrics of
the largest within-metric first-order index --- and cannot be read off the
table's columns.

\input{tables/strata}

\textbf{Most of the pooled surface's variance involves the evaluation
split.} Table~\ref{tab:strata} and Figure~\ref{fig:sobol} partition the
decomposition by \eqref{eq:strata}, exactly and on every decomposition separately. Taking each
column's median across the corpus --- \emph{four medians, which do not add,
and are not four parts of one pair} --- the components involving the split
carry \shareResamplingStratumPct\ of the variance, against
\shareAnalystStratumPct\ for the choices an analyst makes,
\shareCounterfactualStratumPct\ for the register-quality counterfactual and
\shareMixedStratumPct\ for the components that join them. Resampling is the
largest of the four on \nResamplingLargest\ of the \nPairs\ pairs.
That share has a benchmark. With six split levels, the components involving
the split hold \splitNullShareMinPct\ to \splitNullShareMaxPct\ of the
degrees of freedom, so a surface with no signal, whose cells differ only by
independent noise, would put about that share there; the observed share is
below it on \nPairsSplitBelowNull\ of the \nPairs\ pairs, and the analyst's
choices carry \shareAnalystStratumPct\ against the \analystNullSharePct\ such
a surface would give them. The split stratum is where the cells' estimation
noise lands, and a pooled surface that reads it as specification sensitivity
overstates the forecaster's latitude; that is why the decomposition
separates it.
Resampling the pairs puts that median in
[\shareResamplingStratumLoPct, \shareResamplingStratumHiPct] --- an interval
over which pairs make up the corpus, not over sampling error within a pair ---
and it is similar across metrics: in AUC alone it is \splitShareAucPct, in
Brier skill alone \splitShareBrierPct. The split's main effect carries a median
\splitMainPct\ and its interactions with the other axes \splitInteractionsPct,
so most of the stratum is interaction: effects of the other axes that change
from fold to fold, which this design cannot separate from cell-specific
estimation error. The fold-to-fold variation also trends in the fold index
more than exchangeable folds would, which is consistent with the time
structure of an expanding window --- a growing training set and a moving test
period --- and with serially correlated estimation error, which the design
cannot tell apart (Supplement~\ref{app:ijf-foldtrend}). Dropping
the one split level that is a genuine analyst choice --- the single temporal
holdout, leaving five folds of one rule --- the share falls only to
\shareResamplingRollingPct, so the stratum is not an artefact of mixing a
rule with its folds. The two exceptions are the case study's own pairs, where
the analyst axes carry the richest grids in the corpus ---
\nPipelineLevelsCase\ pipelines and \nQualityFigOne\ register-quality
conditions, against at most \nPipelineLevelsLarge\ and \nQualityLevelsOther\
on every other pair --- and the ordering reverses.

\textbf{Net of resampling, no single axis dominates --- a weaker statement
than the pooled surface suggests.} On the fold-averaged surface
(Table~\ref{tab:foldavg}), which carries \foldVarRatioPct\ of the pooled
variance, the median first-order index is \foldSRungPct\ for the baseline,
\foldSQualityPct\ for the register-quality condition and \foldSPipelinePct\
for the pipeline, and at the median pair the largest within-metric
first-order index (a median over metrics) is \foldLargestFirstOrderPct. The total higher-order share
is \foldInteractionTotalPct\ once resampling is removed, against
\pooledInteractionMedianPct\ on the pooled surface. The higher-order share
exceeds the largest within-metric first-order index on
\nPooledInteractionExceeds\ of the \nPairs\ pairs before resampling is
removed, and on \nFoldInteractionExceeds\ after. Counting the five
folds as five specifications makes the interactions look dominant; treated as
resamples, they are not. What remains is that which axis leads is
a property of the pair --- the baseline on \nFoldLeadPairsRung, the
register-quality condition on \nFoldLeadPairsQuality\ and the pipeline on
\nFoldLeadPairsPipeline\ --- so an analyst cannot learn from somebody else's
paper which of their own choices their answer turns on.

The pooled decomposition is reported beside it because it is what the
specification-curve literature computes (Supplement~\ref{app:measures}). The
measure is not neutral: across the three product measures the higher-order
share moves by \measureSpreadPts, and under the measure concentrated on the
reference cell the interactions no longer carry more than the largest
first-order index.

\textbf{The baseline moves the answer by more than the answer, on the pairs
where an analyst has more than one baseline to choose between.} Holding every
other axis at the reference and varying only the baseline, the increment
moves by \spreadMedian\ AUC at the median pair and \spreadMax\ at the widest
--- larger than the increment itself on \nSpreadExceedsIncrement\ of the
\nPairs\ pairs. Without the half-of-intake baseline, which is impoverished in
the way the excluded intercept-only baseline is, the spread is
\spreadRealisticMedian\ AUC at the median pair and \spreadRealisticMax\ at
the widest, exceeding the reference increment on \nSpreadExceedsRealistic\
pairs. On \nPairsTwoRealisticRungs\ of the \nPairs\ pairs only two baselines
then remain, so that spread \emph{is} the absorption $D$ of the free field
(Section~\ref{sec:absorption}; Supplement~\ref{app:spread}). The pipeline
axis is itself two axes, a model family and an encoding, and which of the two
leads is again a property of the pair (Supplement~\ref{app:axes2full}).

\subsection{Resolution regions, and the coverage they have}
\label{sec:regions}

Table~\ref{tab:master} carries the region labels and the robustness index
for every pair (Table~\ref{tab:regions} and Figure~\ref{fig:regions} give the
detail), computed on the inference surface under the whole-surface band at
the empirical critical value. That family has \familySizeMedian\
members on every pair and a median critical value of \maxTSurfaceMedian, against
\maxTWithinMedian\ for the within-cell family; under the narrower family the
same draws give a different label on \nRegionChangedByFamily\ of the
\nPairs\ pairs, and resolve \nResolvedWithin\ cells against the whole-surface
family's \nResolvedWhole\ (summed over pairs).

The multiplier approximation would resolve \nResolvedWholeMult\ cells at a
median robustness index of \rhoMedianMult\ against \rhoMedian\ here, but it
undercovers (Section~\ref{sec:simband}): the \nResolvedLostToEmp\ extra
cells it would resolve are resolved below nominal coverage, so they are not
counted here. Two shortfalls remain and are stated rather than corrected: the
pointwise interval underneath the band undercovers where $K/n$ is large ---
the median pair sits at \medianKoverN\ --- and Supplement~\ref{app:calibration}
reports the labels under an $(n, K)$ widening as a sensitivity only; and the
coverage measurement's bootstrap is ideal, so whatever the real resampling
adds is outside it.

\textbf{Of the \nPairsResolvingAny\ pairs that resolve anything,
\nPairsResolveOneSign\ resolve cells of one sign only ---
\nPairsResolveBeneficialOnly\ beneficial and
\nPairsResolveHarmfulOnly\ harmful --- and
\nPairsResolveBothSigns\ resolve cells of both.} That is the informative
reading of the region column: on \nPairsResolveOneSign\ of the
\nPairsResolvingAny\ pairs where the data determine any sign at all, every
sign they determine points the same way. Part of the both-signs reading is
disagreement between metrics rather than across the surface: on BPIC19 the
resolved cells share one sign within each metric --- \nBpicNineteenBenAuc\
beneficial in AUC, \nBpicNineteenHarmBrier\ harmful in Brier skill --- and
split by metric, the pooled band's cells would carry a different label on
\nPairsLabelDiffersAny\ of the \nPairs\ pairs in at least one metric
(Table~\ref{tab:metricregions}).

\textbf{One surface of the \nPairs\ is uniformly beneficial, over a family
that is \shareUniformFamilyPct\ of that pair's admissible cells.} $\rho = 1$
obtains only on that pair, the one carrying the richest
declared grid, over an inference family of \nCellsPerPairMedian\ cells
against that pair's \nCellsUniformPair\ admissible ones; a claim about every
cell is easier to sustain over fewer of them, so the one case in which a
single number is safe is a claim about \shareUniformFamilyPct\ of the
specifications an analyst could have chosen. The label is not an artefact of
the critical value: it holds under the multiplier band, the reported band,
that band's order-statistic upper end and every $(n, K)$ widening
(Supplement~\ref{app:foursettings} and Supplement~\ref{app:uniformly}).

\subsection{What one number costs}
\label{sec:results-regret}

A conventional report disagrees in \emph{sign} with a median
\misreportEqualPct\ of the other admissible specifications under the
equal-level measure. This rate overstates the risk of a one-number report,
for three reasons that we apply cumulatively: not every axis is an analyst
choice, most cells are not resolved, and many disagreements are too small to
matter.

\textbf{The axes are not all the same kind of thing.}
Table~\ref{tab:axiskind} sums first-order indices into \emph{analyst
latitude} (the pipeline and the baseline), \emph{resampling} (the split) and
the \emph{counterfactual} (the register-quality condition), with the
higher-order remainder a fourth column. Within each metric, analyst latitude
carries a median \shareLatitudePct\ of the variance, resampling
\shareResamplingPct\ and the counterfactual \shareCounterfactualPct, with
resampling the larger on \nPairsResamplingOverLatitude\ of the \nPairs\
pairs; on a common headroom scale the ordering reverses, so neither ordering
is a corpus fact independent of the scale (Supplement~\ref{app:decompmeasure}).
Restricted to the analyst-latitude cells --- register clean, split at the
single holdout, a median \nCellsLatitude\ cells per pair --- the median
disagreement rate is \misreportLatitudePct, and that is what a one-number
report exposes a reader to. Across \nRefSensVariants\ choices of the cell
called conventional, the all-cells rate runs from \refSensMinPct\ to
\refSensMaxPct\ (Table~\ref{tab:refsens}).

\textbf{And most cells are not cells whose sign the data determine.} The
median pair resolves \nResolvedMedian\ cells of the \nCellsPerPairMedian\ it
carries, and sign agreement between two draws from a distribution centred
near zero is one half by construction. \textbf{And a sign flip between two
increments that both round to zero is not a misstatement anybody would act
on}: \nRefBelowMpid\ of the \nPairs\ reference increments are themselves
smaller than \mpidAuc\ AUC in absolute value, so a \emph{minimal practically important
difference} is declared --- $\mathrm{MPID} = \mpidAuc$ AUC --- and a
disagreement is counted only when the reference increment and the cell's own
increment both exceed it, on the AUC sub-surface, because Remark~\ref{prop:nonid}
forbids carrying an AUC threshold to another metric
(Table~\ref{tab:mpid}).

\textbf{On the cells the corpus can resolve, the rate is
\misreportPooledResolvedPct, and it is not an AUC statement.} Of the
\nResolvedCorpus\ cells whose whole-surface band excludes
zero, \nDisagreeResolved\ disagree in sign with the reference cell. Split by
metric that rate runs \misreportResolvedInstrumentMinPct\ on
\instrumentLeastDisagreement\ to \misreportResolvedInstrumentMaxPct\ on
\instrumentMostDisagreement: the pooled figure is carried by the metrics
that are not the one the reference cell is read in, which is
Remark~\ref{prop:nonid} showing up in the corpus. Read against the reference
cell of each cell's own metric instead, \nDisagreeOwnMetric\ of the
\nResolvedCorpus\ cells disagree, \misreportOwnMetricPct; all
\nLeaveBpicNineteenProper\ cells that leave the count are BPIC19's Brier,
log-loss and Nagelkerke cells, on a pair whose resolved AUC cells are beneficial and
whose resolved Brier-skill cells are harmful
(Supplement~\ref{app:ijf-samemetric}).

\textbf{Imposing all three restrictions leaves \nCellsFullyRestricted\ cells,
of which \nDisagreeFullyRestricted\ disagree.} Restrict, on the AUC
sub-surface, to the axes an analyst chooses, to the cells the band resolves,
and to increments that clear the MPID at both ends. Only the
\nInferenceCells\ of the \nCellsAuc\ admissible AUC cells that carry
bootstrap draws can be restricted this way; \nCellsFullyRestricted\ survive,
and the rate is \misreportFullyRestrictedPct. \textbf{We therefore do not
claim that a one-number report misstates a sign at a rate worth quoting as a
headline.} Two corollaries follow, and neither is comfortable for the
conventional report. First, few cells remain because \emph{the data resolve
so little}: \nCellsResolvedAuc\ of the \nInferenceCells\ banded AUC cells
resolve, \shareResolvedAucPct\ of them, and \nRefUnresolvedCell\ of the
\nPairs\ pairs do not resolve the reference cell itself. Second, the surface
still moves, by a distance that dwarfs the MPID: over the cells the band
resolves, the mean distance from the reference increment is a median
\madResolvedMedian\ AUC over the \nPairsMadResolved\ pairs that resolve any AUC
cell, and \madFullyRestrictedMedian\ under the full restriction. The
one-number report is seldom wrong about the sign; what it does not show is
how far the rest of the declared surface lies from it.

\subsection{Comparison with specification-curve analysis}
\label{sec:sca}

Specification-curve analysis \citep{simonsohn2020specification} enumerates
the reasonable specifications, plots the curve, and tests it against a sharp
null by permutation. Run unchanged on every admitted pair
(Supplement~\ref{app:sca}), it parts company with the region label on
\nScaDisagree\ of the \nScaPairs\ pairs: the permutation test rejects the
sharp null and the same cells contain increments of \emph{both} signs. The
permutation test answers \emph{is there anything here}; the region answers
\emph{may I quote a sign}, which is what a forecast user deciding whether to
act needs, and the two should be reported together.
