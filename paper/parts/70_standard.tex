
\section{A reporting standard, and software}
\label{sec:standard}

\subsection{The minimum reportable form}

The apparatus of Sections~\ref{sec:surface} and~\ref{sec:inference} implies a
reporting standard, and it is short enough to state in full. A feature-value
claim is reportable when it carries:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{the design space}, as an enumerable declaration: which learners,
encodings, targets, splits, decision times, quality mechanisms, baselines,
metrics and operating points are admissible, and why anything defensible was
left out;
\item \textbf{the absolute increments} at every rung of the baseline ladder,
in the metric's own units, with intervals from a resampling scheme that
refits the model;
\item \textbf{the sensitivity decomposition}, so a reader can see which of the
analyst's choices their own disagreement would run through;
\item \textbf{the resolution region and $\rho$}, so a reader knows whether the
sign is robust, conditional, or unresolved;
\item \textbf{the ratio $R$ last if at all}, with a Fieller set and the set's
kind, and never where the denominator is not resolvably positive.
\end{enumerate}

Nothing in that list is expensive once the surface exists. The cost is the
surface, and the surface is a factorial over declarations the analyst has
already made implicitly.

\subsection{\texttt{fieldvalue}}
\label{sec:tool}

The standard ships as \texttt{fieldvalue}, a package that computes the surface
from a data frame, a feature name and a dictionary of baselines. Its notable
property is a refusal: \texttt{Surface} raises rather than returning a scalar,
because \texttt{float(surface)} would be an argument against the paper it
accompanies. \texttt{Surface.report()} emits the minimum reportable form
above; \texttt{Surface.at(...)} returns one cell and requires the caller to
name every axis, so standing somewhere on the surface is a deliberate act.

Version \fvVersion\ adds the four objects of
Sections~\ref{sec:sobol}--\ref{sec:regretdef}
as functions of a surface --- \texttt{decompose}, \texttt{regions},
\texttt{robustness}, \texttt{regret} --- so the standard the paper states is
the standard the package implements, and each takes a tidy frame rather than
this package's own object, so a reader can apply them to a surface computed by
some other pipeline.

The package carries \nTests\ tests. It is also an \emph{independent
implementation}: the analysis scripts and the package compute the same four
objects from the same definitions in different code, neither written against
the other, and \texttt{scripts/s11\_tool\_agreement.py} runs both on the same
input. On this paper's corpus they agree on \nAgreeQuantities\ of
\nAgreeQuantities\ quantities, with a largest difference of \agreeTolerance.
That check matters because twice in this project's history the two disagreed,
and both times the paper was wrong and the package was right.


\subsection{A worked example that is not a process log}
\label{sec:worked}

The standard is not about event logs and not about configuration databases,
and the quickest way to show that is to run it on something else. UCI Adult
(Census Income) predicts whether a person's income exceeds a threshold from
\exRows\ records. Suppose an analyst wants to report what \texttt{occupation}
is worth. It is the expensive field: a survey has to ask about it and code it,
where age, sex, race and country come with the sampling frame and education
comes with an enrolment record.

Worth compared with what? Against the frame alone, against the frame plus
education, or against the frame plus education plus hours worked. Measured
how, at which operating point, and with the register how complete. Over the
resulting \exCells\ cells the reduction runs \exRmin\ to \exRmax, the baseline
axis carries \exSbaselinePct\ of the variance in the increment against
\exSmetricPct\ for the metric and \exSpopulationPct\ for the population, and a
one-number report misstates the sign for a mean \exMisreportPct\ of the other
cells.

\texttt{examples/worked\_example.py} produces all of it in \exampleLines\
statements against the package's public interface, and ends by calling
\texttt{float()} on the surface so a reader can see what the refusal looks
like.

\subsection{How the surface is reported today: a measured pilot}
\label{sec:practice}

\input{tables/pilot}

We wanted to know whether the axes this paper varies are varied in published
work. That question deserves a systematic review, and what follows is not one.
It is a \textbf{machine-assisted prevalence pilot} with a measured error rate,
reported here with everything that limits it, and \emph{no claim in this paper
depends on it}.

The design is two-stratum, and the flow follows the PRISMA 2020 reporting
items \citep{page2021prisma} where they apply to a pilot of this kind. The
enumeration --- venue queries, two citation seeds and a topic frame ---
returned \nAuditFrame\ deduplicated open-access records across \nAuditStrata\
strata, well short of the \nAuditTarget\ the protocol budgeted, because the
provider's daily request budget was exhausted before the venue queries were
exhausted. \textbf{Every enumerated record was therefore taken}: the design
weight is \auditMaxWeight\ in every stratum, \nAuditSampled\ records were
coded, and the pilot is a \emph{census of what the enumeration returned}
rather than a probability subsample of a literature. That is a smaller object
than a stratified sample of the field, and it is what we have; an earlier
version of this section quoted a frame two orders of magnitude larger,
which was a sum taken over rows of a table whose stratum size is repeated on
every row. Full text was retrieved for \nAuditFullText. A mechanical screen, whose rules
were fixed before the first paper was read, selected \nAuditScreenedIn\ as
reporting the incremental value of a named feature. Every one of those was
then adjudicated against its full text, and a random \nAuditOutSampled-paper
subsample of the \nAuditScreenedOut\ the screen rejected was adjudicated too,
so that the screen's \emph{misses} could be counted rather than assumed away.

The screen's measured accuracy is the pilot's most useful output and its most
damaging. Sensitivity is \auditSens\ [\auditSensLo, \auditSensHi]: the
mechanical rule finds fewer than half of the eligible papers. Specificity is
\auditSpec\ and precision \auditPrec, so about half of what it selects is not
in scope. Agreement between the mechanical codes and the adjudicated ones has
median $\kappa = \auditKappa$. \textbf{A coder with that error rate cannot
support a claim about a field's practice}, and an earlier version of this work
made one. A classifier-error correction of the Rogan--Gladen kind
\citep{rogan1978estimating} would repair the point estimate but not its
interval; the two-stratum design used here does better, and lets the
prevalence itself be estimated without bias, because the eligible papers the screen missed are counted and
weighted rather than ignored, and Table~\ref{tab:pilot} reports the result on
that basis.

What it says, over an estimated \nAuditEligible\ eligible papers with an
effective sample size of \nAuditEffective: \auditBStatedPct\ state the
composition of their baseline; \auditBJustifiedPct\ argue for what the
baseline excludes; \auditMJustifiedPct\ argue for the metric;
\auditThetaStatedPct\ state or integrate over an operating point;
\auditRangeBaselinePct\ report the increment at more than one baseline,
\auditRangeMetricPct\ under more than one metric, \auditRangeThresholdPct\
across a range of operating points and \auditRangePopulationPct\ across a
range of register populations. Of the eligible papers only \nAuditApplicable\
have an incremental feature that is a lookup into an external register at all,
so the population axis has a denominator of \nAuditApplicable\ and not of
\nAuditEligible: an applicability-specific denominator, which the earlier
version did not use.

Four limits, stated rather than buried. The frame is what one provider's
index returned before its daily quota stopped the enumeration, so it is a
convenience frame and its coverage of the field is unmeasured; nothing below
corrects for that, and nothing can without a second index. An effective sample
of
\nAuditEffective\ resolves a proportion to about $\pm\auditHalfWidthPct$
percentage points; a design that resolved it to $\pm 7$ points would need
\nAuditRequired\ adjudicated papers. Full text was unavailable for \nAuditNoFullText\ of the
sampled records, and under the extreme assumptions that none or all of them
are eligible the eligibility rate moves between \auditBoundLo\ and
\auditBoundHi. And there is one adjudicator, machine-assisted, not two
independent human raters; every adjudication was made from an evidence dossier
that is committed to the repository, so the judgements can be re-examined
against exactly what they were made from, but that is transparency and not
independence.

The correct reading of the pilot is therefore narrow: \emph{in a sample of
this size, with a coder of this accuracy, we did not find papers reporting an
increment across register populations, and found few reporting one across
operating points}. That is a hypothesis about practice, sized and bounded. It
is not a finding about the field.
