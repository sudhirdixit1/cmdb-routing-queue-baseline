\begin{abstract}
Forecast evaluation asks whether a predictor improves out-of-sample
probability forecasts over a nested benchmark, answering with one score
difference dependent on unstated choices: the benchmark, model class,
split, forecast origin, predictor completeness and scoring rule. We treat
the improvement as a surface over declared choices, with three
reporting objects: an exact variance decomposition separating
forecaster choices from split-to-split variation; simultaneous bootstrap
bands, their family-wise coverage measured by simulation; and resolution
regions, where its sign is determined. Across \nPairs\ binary forecasting tasks from
\nLogs\ public event logs, mostly IT-service and
administrative, the evaluation split and its interactions carry a median
\shareResamplingStratumPct\ of the variance, and resolved specifications (\nPairsMadResolved\ tasks) lie a
median \madResolvedMedian\ in area under the ROC curve from the conventional
report. In a bank
service-desk case study, the predictor improves reassignment forecasts at
first touch, not at incident creation, once the benchmark holds what is
assumed recorded by then.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Does a candidate predictor improve a forecast? Forecast evaluation answers by
comparing nested models out of sample --- a benchmark with and without the
predictor \citep{clark2001nested,clark2007approximately} --- and reports the
answer as one number, the difference in a score. That number is conditional
on choices the forecaster makes and seldom reports: the benchmark information
set, the model class and its encoding, the forecast target, the sample split,
the \emph{forecast origin} (the time at which the forecast is issued, which
fixes what is known about a case), how completely the predictor is recorded,
and the score and operating point at which the difference is read. Several
of these have been studied one at a time --- the estimation window
\citep{rossi2012window}, the sample split \citep{hansen2012sample}, the
accuracy measure \citep{kolassa2020best} --- and applied work finds that
whether a predictor helps can turn on the sample and the specification that
carry it \citep{stock2003forecasting,welch2008comprehensive,banbura2023phillips}. What is missing is a way to report how a
predictor's incremental forecast performance varies jointly over all of them,
with uncertainty that holds jointly over a declared family of specifications. This paper supplies one
for probability forecasts of binary events.

The application is service-operations forecasting. A service desk decides,
as each case arrives, where to route it, and a case that is later passed to
another team, or that stays open far longer than usual, costs the desk time
and service levels. We forecast those two events --- \emph{reassignment}
(handover to another team) before resolution, and \emph{long duration} --- as
probabilities issued at a declared origin, and ask whether a field from the
organisation's inventory of the things its cases concern, called here the
\emph{register}, improves the forecast over the case's arrival attributes. In
IT service management that inventory is the configuration management
database, and in the case study of Section~\ref{sec:cmdb} the candidate
predictor is the configuration item an incident affects. Service desks sit
beside the call centres and emergency departments whose operations
forecasters already model \citep{ibrahim2016modeling,rostamitabar2022anticipating},
and their case-level event forecasts are of the kind made for customer churn
and credit default \citep{decaigny2020incorporating,liu2023tree}.

Our position is that the dependence on unstated choices is large enough, on
real forecasting decisions, that the number alone is not a report; that the
right object is a surface over a \emph{declared} design space; and that the
surface admits estimation and inference of its own. Two findings follow.
\textbf{A pooled specification surface overstates the forecaster's
latitude}: most of its variance here comes from the evaluation split and its
interactions --- sampling variability and fold-to-fold instability ---
rather than from the forecaster's other choices, and an exact decomposition
partitions the two
(Section~\ref{sec:sobol}). And \textbf{where the data determine the sign of
the improvement, the conventional report usually has the right sign but does
not show how far other defensible specifications lie from it};
Section~\ref{sec:results-regret} measures that distance.

\paragraph{The estimand}
Write a specification $s$ for a complete assignment to every design axis, and
\begin{equation}
V_s(f) \;=\; m_\theta\!\left(A_s(D_{\mathrm{train}}, B \cup \{f_q\}),\,
D_{\mathrm{test}}\right)
\;-\;
m_\theta\!\left(A_s(D_{\mathrm{train}}, B),\, D_{\mathrm{test}}\right),
\label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
where $A_s$ is the fitting procedure (learner, encoding, hyperparameters,
calibration), $B$ the baseline feature set, $f_q$ the field under a register
quality mechanism $q$, $m$ the metric and $\theta$ the operating point, and
where $D_{\mathrm{train}}, D_{\mathrm{test}}$ are fixed by the split rule and
the cohort. The \emph{specification surface} is the map
$s \mapsto V_s(f)$ over a design space $\mathcal{S}$ that the analyst declares
and the reader can enumerate; Figure~\ref{fig:speccurve} draws one.
Equation~\eqref{eq:estimand} measures how much better a model forecasts, not what the
field costs or what acting on the forecast is worth; Section~\ref{sec:dca}
converts the increment into net benefit at a declared exchange rate, the
sense in which \citet{murphy1993good} and \citet{granger2000economic} speak of
a forecast's value.

\paragraph{What kind of forecast this is}
Every forecast here is a case-level probability forecast of a binary event
--- that a case will be handed over to another team, or will stay open longer
than the training period's median --- issued once, at a declared forecast
origin, from the information recorded by then, as in churn, credit-default
and recession-probability forecasting \citep{liu2016recessions}. It is scored
on a later hold-out period by \nInstruments\ declared scalar metrics, the area
under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve first among them, and in Section~\ref{sec:dca} by net
benefit at a declared operating point. The objects that follow apply to any
increment in a score between two nested forecasting pipelines, with or
without a time-series structure; the moving-block bootstrap that produces the
bands is used because cases arrive in time order.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An estimand for nested forecast comparisons that includes the
evaluation design.} The incremental score as a function of the benchmark,
the model class, the sample split, the forecast origin, the predictor's data
quality and the scoring rule, over a design space the reader can enumerate
(Table~\ref{tab:axes}); and an exact functional-ANOVA decomposition of its
variance in which the evaluation split is an \emph{error stratum}, so that
the evaluation split, with its sampling variability and fold-to-fold
instability, is separated from the forecaster's other choices
(Section~\ref{sec:sobol}).
\item \textbf{Simultaneous inference over a declared family.} Max-$t$ bands
from a bootstrap that refits the forecasting models in every draw, at a
critical value that attains \covEmpHeavyWholeAtDesign\ family-wise coverage
in a simulation matched to this design (Section~\ref{sec:simband}) --- an
upper bound, because that simulation's bootstrap is ideal --- and \emph{resolution
regions}, which state where the sign of the improvement is determined, with a
robustness index $\rho$ under a declared minimum resolved share. Along the
way, refitting a high-cardinality predictor inside each draw is shown in
simulation to break the percentile bootstrap interval, which is why every
interval here is the basic one (Section~\ref{sec:boot}).
\item \textbf{Evidence on \nPairs\ pre-specified forecasting tasks and a
service-desk case study in which the forecast origin is part of the
estimand.} One frozen pipeline is run on every log--target pair a pre-specified
protocol admits, not only those where the answer resolves, and every corpus
statistic is reported on all \nPairs\ and on the \nItsmPairs\ IT-service
pairs (Supplement~\ref{sec:family}). In the case study
(Section~\ref{sec:cmdb}) the predictor's increment is large when the
reassignment forecast is issued at first touch and indistinguishable from
zero at incident creation once the benchmark holds the fields assumed recorded
by then; a decision-based evaluation prices what reading the wrong benchmark
costs a desk (Section~\ref{sec:nbregret}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

In the forecasting literature the incremental question is asked of one
evaluation design at a time. Whether one forecast is more accurate than
another is tested by \citet{diebold1995comparing} and
\citet{west1996asymptotic}; whether a predictor adds accuracy to a model that
nests the one without it --- the incremental question, exactly --- by
\citet{clark2001nested}, and \citet{clark2007approximately} show that under
the null, estimating the larger model's extra parameters inflates its
out-of-sample mean squared error, biasing the raw comparison against it;
whether it adds accuracy \emph{conditionally}, at a given origin and state,
by \citet{giacomini2006conditional}. \citet{gonzalo2024outofsample} extend
nested out-of-sample comparison to many candidate predictors at once, and
\citet{liu2024marginal} measure the marginal predictive content of predictor
groups across targets and horizons; both vary the predictor and hold the
evaluation design fixed, where this paper holds the predictor fixed and
varies the design. Probability forecasts of a binary event are evaluated in
the area under the ROC curve \citep{lahiri2013evaluating}, in which a
predictor's increment over a nested baseline is read
\citep{liu2016recessions} and tests of that increment are badly behaved at
the null \citep{demler2012misuse,pepe2013testing} --- one reason the
increments here are reported as estimates with intervals, although deciding
a sign still operates at that null --- and in proper scores
\citep{brier1950verification,gneiting2007scoring}, whose decomposition
separates the calibration an AUC ignores from the discrimination it measures
\citep{dimitriadis2021stable}. Reliability, ROC and Murphy curves together
separate calibration, discrimination and value when probabilistic
classifiers are compared \citep{dimitriadis2024triptych}; this paper evaluates the
increment between two nested forecasts across a design, and
Section~\ref{sec:dca} reads that increment in net benefit.

Several of the choices this paper puts on a surface have been studied one at
a time: the estimation window, over which \citet{rossi2012window} make tests
robust; the sample split, whose choice \citet{hansen2012sample} show can
manufacture rejections, and whose place in the limit theory of nested
comparisons \citet{hansen2015equivalence} characterise through Wald
statistics; the forecast origin, along which \citet{giacomini2010unstable}
track relative performance and which rolling-origin evaluation varies by
design \citep{tashman2000outofsample}; and the accuracy measure, which can
change which forecast is best \citep{hyndman2006another,kolassa2020best}.
Applied forecasters meet the joint dependence: whether the Phillips curve
helps forecast euro-area inflation turns on the specification chosen, and
averaging over modelling choices helps \citep{banbura2023phillips}.
\citet{petropoulos2022forecasting} and \citet{hewamalage2023forecast} collect
the evaluation practice these rest on. When the comparison ranges over a
\emph{family} of specifications, the reality check \citep{white2000reality},
the test for superior predictive ability \citep{hansen2005spa} and the model
confidence set \citep{hansen2011mcs} control the search: the first two ask
whether the best member of a family beats a benchmark once the search is
accounted for, the third returns the set of members not rejected as inferior
at a declared level; and \citet{hunt2022insample} shows that pseudo-out-of-sample
tests do not keep a stable family-wise error rate when models are searched
for. This paper sits beside the family tests and takes a different object
from them: not whether the family contains a member that beats the
benchmark, nor which members survive, but the shape of a fixed predictor's
increment across the family --- how much of its variation is the
forecaster's and how much is resampling, where its sign is determined, and
how far its members sit from the one that would conventionally be reported.
A forecaster's usual answer to many specifications is to combine them
\citep{wang2023combinations}; this paper asks the prior question of how much
they disagree, and where. Forecasting also has precedents for working over the
specification space rather than at a single specification: recursive selection over
every subset of candidate regressors at each forecast origin, to ask whether
predictability survives the search \citep{pesaran1995predictability}, and
thick modelling, which keeps and combines the forecasts of many
specifications instead of selecting one
\citep{granger2004thick,aiolfi2005model}. Both use the space to produce a
forecast; this paper uses it to report how an increment varies across it.

What a forecast is worth to a decision, as distinct from how accurate it is,
is the subject of \citet{murphy1993good} and \citet{granger2000economic}, and
the with-versus-without question is asked of many objects: of a dataset,
through a betting market \citep{maymin2019wage}; of a new data source in
customer-churn forecasting, in accuracy and in campaign profit
\citep{decaigny2020incorporating}; of a member of a forecast ensemble, by
leave-one-out and Shapley differences \citep{kim2026beyond}; of individual
predictors, whose contributions to out-of-sample loss a performance-based
Shapley value attributes \citep{borup2022anatomy}; and of a step in a
forecasting process, as forecast value added \citep{fildes2025forecast}. Each
answers under one evaluation design; none asks how the answer moves when the
design moves. The clinical-prediction literature has argued for two decades
that the increment in the area under the ROC curve from adding a marker
depends on the existing model and is a poor guide to usefulness
\citep{pencina2008evaluating,cook2007roc,kerr2011evaluating}, and decision
curves read net benefit across an exchange rate
\citep{vickers2006dca,vickers2016net}, which is why the operating point is an
axis here. Multiverse and specification-curve analysis
\citep{steegen2016increasing,simonsohn2020specification} supply the sweep over
analytic choices from which the robustness index below is derived.
Incompletely recorded inputs are a forecasting problem in their own right
\citep{wen2024wind}; the register-quality axis puts the predictor's
completeness inside the estimand rather than in preprocessing. And the
forecast origin turns what machine learning treats as leakage
\citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage} into a declared choice of
information set. Variable importance and data valuation, underspecification
and predictive multiplicity, many-analysts studies, and the business-process
analytics literature from which the data come, where such case-level
forecasts are called outcome-oriented predictive monitoring
\citep{teinemaa2019outcome}, are discussed in Supplement~\ref{app:related}.

\paragraph{What is new for forecast evaluation}
Forecast-comparison tests either fix the evaluation design and ask whether a
predictor, or the best of a family of models, improves accuracy
\citep{clark2001nested,hansen2005spa,gonzalo2024outofsample}, or robustify
one design choice at a time \citep{rossi2012window,hansen2012sample}. This
paper reports how a fixed predictor's incremental accuracy varies jointly
across the design, separates the evaluation split's share of that variation
from the forecaster's other choices, and gives inference that holds jointly
over a declared family. Three things are
new. The estimand is declared completely: the forecast origin and the
predictor's data quality are arguments of it rather than preliminaries, and
the measure over the design is stated rather than implied by whichever cells
were enumerated, which moves the answer (Section~\ref{sec:axes2}). The
decomposition treats the evaluation split as an error stratum and so
partitions the forecaster's choices from the split's exactly; separating
specification-induced from sampling variation has precedents ---
extreme-bounds analysis, which reports an estimate's range across
specifications \citep{leamer1983con}, multimodel analysis that attributes an
estimate's variation to each modelling choice \citep{young2017model},
functional ANOVA over a configuration space \citep{hutter2014efficient}, and
the error strata of classical experimental design --- and what is added is
its exact application to a nested out-of-sample forecast comparison, with
the evaluation split as the stratum. And the share of specifications with an
improvement in a declared direction, which is standard specification-curve
practice \citep{simonsohn2020specification} and which the robustness index
$\rho$ counts, is taken under a \emph{simultaneous} band over a declared
family rather than a pointwise test, over a counted set of specifications,
and subject to a minimum resolved share below which no direction is reported;
Section~\ref{sec:sca} and Supplement~\ref{app:sca} measure where a
specification curve's verdict and a region label part company, and
Section~\ref{sec:results-regret} measures how far the conventional report
sits from the resolved specifications. The contribution is not the
observation that a single number is usually reported. It is that an
incremental-performance claim is a functional of a declared design, that the
functional can be estimated with quantified uncertainty, and that on this
corpus it varies widely.

\section{The specification surface}
\label{sec:surface}

This section defines the object. A forecaster comparing two nested
forecasting pipelines --- one with the candidate predictor, one without ---
reports the difference in a score; the surface is that difference as a
function of every choice the comparison rests on, and the design space is
the set of those choices, declared before the comparison is run.

\subsection{The design space}

\begin{definition}[Design space]
\label{def:space}
A \emph{design space} $\mathcal{S}$ for an incremental-performance claim is a finite set
of specifications, each a complete assignment to the axes
\[
\begin{aligned}
s \;=\; \bigl(\;
&\underbrace{A}_{\text{learner}},\;
\underbrace{E}_{\text{encoding}},\;
\underbrace{Y}_{\text{target}},\;
\underbrace{\Pi}_{\text{split}},\;
\underbrace{\tau}_{\text{forecast origin}},\; \\[2pt]
&\underbrace{q}_{\text{register quality}},\;
\underbrace{B}_{\text{baseline}},\;
\underbrace{m}_{\text{metric}},\;
\underbrace{\theta}_{\text{operating point}}\;\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
together with the cohort and eligibility rule that fix $D$. A claim about a
feature is \emph{well posed} only relative to a declared $\mathcal{S}$.
\end{definition}

\noindent The baselines are nested, $B_0 \subset B_1 \subset \cdots$, and we
call them the \emph{nested baselines}: $B_0$ is the intake block (the
low-cardinality attributes recorded when a case opens), and a richer baseline
adds the \emph{free field} $g$, a routinely recorded attribute --- on the
case-study log the assignment group --- that may carry the same information
as the register $f$. The metric axis has \nInstruments\ levels, the
\emph{metrics}: the area under the ROC curve (AUC), average precision, the
Brier skill score, the log-loss skill score and Nagelkerke's $R^2$, of which
the Brier and log-loss skill scores are affine in a strictly proper score on
a fixed test set. We call the value
$V_s(f)$ at one specification a \emph{cell} of the surface; a \emph{scalar}
cell is one whose metric is one of these scores rather than a point of the
decision curve, and a cell is \emph{admissible} if it lies in the declared
space and no declared rule excludes it (for scalar cells the one such rule
excludes the intercept-only baseline).

\input{tables/axes}

Declaring a design space means writing down every axis, its levels, the
measure over those levels, and every level deliberately excluded with the
reason. Table~\ref{tab:axes} is that declaration. A pair's admissible cell
count is the product of five of its rows --- learner, split, register-quality
condition, admissible baseline and scalar metric; the note to the table says
how the other axes enter. Not all \nAxes\ axes are varied on every pair
(Section~\ref{sec:design} and Section~\ref{sec:lim}). The measure over the
levels is declared rather than left implicit, and the pooled decomposition is
reported under three product measures (Table~\ref{tab:measures};
Supplement~\ref{app:measurechoice}).

\subsection{What the surface estimates}
\label{sec:estimand}

Equation~\eqref{eq:estimand} is written on a fixed $D_{\mathrm{train}}$ and
$D_{\mathrm{test}}$, so as it stands $V_s(f)$ is a deterministic functional of
the log in hand and an interval for it would cover with probability zero or
one. The quantity the intervals of Section~\ref{sec:inference} target is the
following.

\begin{definition}[The estimand]
\label{def:estimand}
Let the cases of a log be a sample from a population $P$, and let the
specification $s$ fix the fitting procedure $A_s$, the cohort and eligibility
rule, the split rule $\Pi$, the forecast origin, the register-quality mechanism,
the baseline and the metric. Write $A_s(P)$ for the limit of the fitted
score function as the sample grows under $P$ with $s$ held fixed. The limit
requires the case sequence to be stationary and mixing, so that a temporal
split does not separate two populations and the moving-block bootstrap of
Section~\ref{sec:boot} resamples a persistent dependence structure;
stationarity is tested and does not hold everywhere --- the increment
differs across the test half on \nDrifts\ of \nDriftPairs\ pairs
(Supplement~\ref{app:drift}) --- and mixing is not tested
(Supplement~\ref{app:threats}). The \emph{estimand at $s$} is
\[
\vartheta_s(f) \;=\; m_\theta\bigl(A_s(P, B \cup \{f_q\}),\, P\bigr)
\;-\; m_\theta\bigl(A_s(P, B),\, P\bigr),
\]
and $V_s(f)$ of~\eqref{eq:estimand} is its plug-in estimate on the log.
\end{definition}

\noindent Three things follow. The split rule and the pipeline are
\emph{part of the estimand}, not nuisances to be averaged away, which is why
the nested bootstrap of Section~\ref{sec:boot} resamples the training half
rather than conditioning on it. Under misspecification $\vartheta_s$ is not
what the register is worth in the world; the simulation of
Section~\ref{sec:sim} separates the two as $V_{\mathrm{limit}}$ and
$V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$. And every cell of every table is an \emph{estimate} of
$\vartheta_s$, so ``resolved'' in Definition~\ref{def:regions} means that the
band excludes zero for $\vartheta_s$, not that the observed increment is
nonzero.

\subsection{The surface, and why it is not a scalar}

Given $\mathcal{S}$ and data $D$, the surface is the map $s \mapsto V_s(f)$
of~\eqref{eq:estimand}. The \emph{conventional report} is one cell of it, the
reference cell: one-hot logistic regression, the single temporal holdout, the
clean register, the intake block plus the free field, and AUC. The paper's
claim is that a single cell is not a summary of the surface in any useful
sense. The point estimates alone establish that: on \nSignVaries\ of the
\nPairs\ pairs, between a tenth and nine tenths of the admissible cells are
positive. Whether that amounts to a \emph{misstatement} is a harder question,
which Section~\ref{sec:results-regret} answers with three restrictions rather
than with a rate. A scalar is defensible in one state: when the whole
admissible surface lies on the same side of zero with simultaneous
confidence, a positive number is a lower bound on a robust conclusion
(Section~\ref{sec:regionsdef}).

Comparisons between two baselines are often stated as a proportion. Write
$B_0 \subset B_1$ and
\begin{equation}
D_s(f) \;=\; V_s(f \mid B_0) - V_s(f \mid B_1),
\quad
R_s(f) \;=\; 1 - \frac{V_s(f \mid B_1)}{V_s(f \mid B_0)}
\;=\; \frac{D_s(f)}{V_s(f \mid B_0)} ;
\label{eq:reduction}
\end{equation}
$D_s(f)$, the \emph{absorption}, is how much of the register's increment the
richer baseline $B_1$ takes up, in the metric's own units. $R$ is a ratio and
inherits every pathology of one, so $D$ and the two increments are primary
and $R$ is a secondary descriptive statistic, reported under three declared
rules with a Fieller set \citep{fieller1954} whose \emph{kind} --- bounded,
unbounded, or the complement of an interval --- is printed beside it
(Supplement~\ref{sec:reduction} and Supplement~\ref{app:fieller}).

\begin{remark}[The metric is an argument of the estimand]
\label{prop:nonid}
No function carries the AUC reduction to the average-precision reduction: two
configurations can share an AUC reduction to \propOneAucGap\ while their
average-precision reductions differ by \propOneApGap. The reduction is
therefore not identified by the data, the feature and the two baselines, which
is why every table in this paper names its metric and why the minimal
practically important difference of Section~\ref{sec:results-regret} is
declared in one metric's units and applied there only. That forecast rankings
can depend on the scoring function is established for point and quantile
forecasts \citep{ehm2016quantiles,patton2020comparing}; the remark is its
counterpart for an increment between nested models.
\end{remark}

\noindent A second property --- Remark~\ref{prop:rank}, that a reduction is
invariant to a declared family of recalibrations exactly when the metric is
\emph{affine} under it --- is stated and proved in
Supplement~\ref{app:props}.

\section{Estimation and inference}
\label{sec:inference}

This section gives the estimator of one cell and its interval, then the
objects that read the surface as a whole: the set a claim ranges over,
simultaneous bands, the variance decomposition, and the regions where a sign
is determined.

\subsection{The estimator, and uncertainty that includes the model}
\label{sec:boot}

The pipeline is frozen and identical on every log, and the \emph{pipeline}
axis spans encodings as well as model families; all models are fitted with
\texttt{scikit-learn} \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Attributes are assigned to roles by the
pre-specified rules of Section~\ref{sec:design}, cases are ordered by their
first timestamp, and the split is temporal (Supplement~\ref{app:pipeline}).
The usual interval for such an increment resamples test rows with the models
held fixed, which excludes training-sample, model-selection, encoder and
split variability and the temporal dependence between cases. Every interval
here is instead a \emph{nested moving-block bootstrap}: one draw resamples
the training half by moving blocks \citep{kunsch1989jackknife} of length
$\lceil n^{1/3} \rceil$, refits both \emph{arms} of every comparison --- the
model with the register and the nested model without it --- under every
baseline, learner and quality mechanism, and evaluates on a moving-block
resample of the test half, with encoders, frequency tables, target
encodings, register rankings and calibrators rebuilt inside the draw. The
nested construction is used because the variability it captures is part of
the estimand, not because it covers better: in the simulation of
Section~\ref{sec:sim} the two intervals cannot be told apart at its Monte
Carlo precision.

\noindent Throughout, $K$ denotes the \textbf{cardinality of the register
field} --- the number of distinct values $f$ takes on a pair, \cardFMedian\ at
the median pair and \cardFMax\ at the largest (Table~\ref{tab:master}) --- and
$n$ the number of training cases. The ratio $K/n$ governs everything in this
subsection and in Section~\ref{sec:regions}.

\paragraph{Refitting inside the draw breaks the percentile interval}
Refitting every arm inside every draw displaces the bootstrap distribution
from the estimate. Part of the cause is that a multinomial resample holds
only about $1 - e^{-1}$ of a high-cardinality categorical's distinct levels,
so a refit sees a sparser register, but removing that loss leaves most of
the displacement standing (next paragraph). A percentile interval taken from
a displaced distribution is a correct interval for the wrong quantity. On the sparse world of Section~\ref{sec:sim} it covers
\coverageSparsePct\ against a nominal $95\%$, \emph{worse} than the
fixed-model interval refitting was meant to improve on. Every interval here
is therefore the \emph{basic} (pivotal) interval
$[\,2\hat{V} - q_{1-\alpha/2},\; 2\hat{V} - q_{\alpha/2}\,]$
\citep{davison1997bootstrap}, and simultaneous bands are recentred by the
same shift (Supplement~\ref{app:pctfail}).

\paragraph{Weights rather than subsets}
Every band comes from a bootstrap that draws Dirichlet \emph{weights} per
moving block rather than a subset of rows, so every register level is present
in every refit and no cell loses its band because a draw dropped an arm ---
\nUnbandableCells\ unbandable cells under weights, against
\nUnbandableSameDesign\ of \nUnbandableSameDesignOf\ under a multinomial
resample of the same design. Weights do not remove the displacement of the
bootstrap distribution; its residual cause is not identified here and is
listed among the limitations (Supplement~\ref{app:schemes} and
Supplement~\ref{app:threats}). One candidate mechanism, not established
here, is that block weights lower the effective training size, so a draw sits
lower on the learning curve that $K/n$ governs than the full-sample estimate
does.

The analysis code, and separate code that re-derives every reported number,
were written with the help of a generative-AI tool (see the declaration
before the references); no reported number depends on the tool being run
again, because the archived code regenerates every result from the public
logs (see the data availability statement).

\subsection{Three surfaces, and which one a claim may quantify over}
\label{sec:threesurfaces}

A statement of the form \emph{this surface is uniformly beneficial} ranges
over a set of specifications, and three sets are distinguished and counted
(Table~\ref{tab:denominator}). The \textbf{declared surface} is every
admissible specification: the product of the axis levels declared in
Section~\ref{sec:design}. The \textbf{computational surface} is every
specification actually evaluated; here it equals the declared surface: all
\nDeclaredAdmissibleScalar\ declared scalar cells were evaluated, with
\nMissingCells\ missing and \nDuplicateCells\ duplicated. The
\textbf{inference surface} is every specification carrying bootstrap draws;
it is smaller because a draw refits the whole pipeline, so its cost is the
declared surface's multiplied by the draw count.

The rule that selects the inference surface reads neither a result nor the
log: one design on every pair --- two learners, two splits, three
register-quality conditions and three admissible baselines, crossed,
\nCellsPerDecomposition\ cells a pair per metric and \nInferenceScalar\
scalar family members across the corpus, at \nDrawsSurface\ draws --- a
complete factorial, identical on every pair (Supplement~\ref{app:pipeline};
Supplement~\ref{app:infanova} compares the decomposition on the two
surfaces). The inference family is a median
\inferenceShareAdmissibleMedianPct\ of a pair's admissible cells, and less
than a twentieth on the pair with the richest grid; \textbf{a label
computed over fewer cells is systematically cleaner than one computed over
all of them} (Section~\ref{sec:regionsdef}). Only the region labels use the
inference surface; the decomposition, the sign-disagreement rate (the share
of cells whose sign differs from the conventional report's) and the regret
comparison use the full declared surface.

\subsection{Simultaneous bands over the family the claim ranges over}
\label{sec:simbands}

A decision curve read across \nGrid\ thresholds, a set of nested baselines
read together, or a surface read across every admissible cell, is a family of
statements, and a pointwise interval does not control the probability that
\emph{some} member of the family is wrong. For a declared family
$\mathcal{F}$ of cells with bootstrap draws $V^{(b)}_c$ and per-cell standard
errors $\hat\sigma_c$, let
\[
T^{(b)} \;=\; \max_{c \in \mathcal{F}}
\frac{\bigl|V^{(b)}_c - \bar{V}_c\bigr|}{\hat\sigma_c},
\qquad
q_{1-\alpha} = \text{the } (1-\alpha)\text{ quantile of } \{T^{(b)}\},
\]
and report $\hat{V}_c \pm q_{1-\alpha}\hat\sigma_c$, recentred by the basic
construction's shift as above. This is the max-$t$
(Romano--Wolf) construction \citep{romano2005stepdown} and has family-wise
coverage over $\mathcal{F}$ asymptotically, and over nothing larger. It
differs from the model confidence set \citep{hansen2011mcs} in what it
returns: the confidence set is the subset of a family not rejected as
inferior to its best member, a statement about \emph{which} specifications
survive; the band is a simultaneous interval for \emph{every} member's
increment, from which a region label reads where the sign is determined and
a robustness index how much of the family agrees. Both rest on a bootstrap
of the maximum over the family; the objects read off it are different.

\paragraph{Which family a band must cover}
Within-cell bands, each covering the \nInstruments\ metrics of one cell, do
not cover the several hundred cells that a surface-level label ranges over.
The \textsc{whole-surface} family --- max-$t$ over \emph{every} scalar cell
of the pair's inference family --- is the only family a surface-level claim
may use; it has \familySizeMedian\ members on every pair against the within-cell
family's \nInstruments, at a median critical value of \maxTSurfaceMedian\
against that family's \maxTWithinMedian\ and the pointwise $1.96$. Family
control is per log--target pair, so every cross-pair statement here is
descriptive; Supplement~\ref{app:families} defines the four interval objects
and the rule for dropping a replicate that leaves a cell without outcome
variation.

\paragraph{Estimating the critical value}
Two estimators of $q_{1-\alpha}$ are computed on every family. The first is
the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of the $B$ observed maxima --- the
Romano--Wolf construction as cited --- whose precision is that of an order
statistic of $B$ values. The second multiplies the standardised draws by
independent standard normals \citep{chernozhukov2013gaussian}, which frees
the precision of $q$ from $B$ but reproduces only the excursions a Gaussian
process makes; on every family here it lies below the empirical quantile's
whole order-statistic interval (Supplement~\ref{app:twoq}). \textbf{The
operative critical value is the empirical quantile}; the multiplier's median
on the whole-surface families is \maxTSurfaceMultMedian, and
Section~\ref{sec:simband} measures the family-wise coverage of both against a
known answer. A max-$t$ band also inherits the coverage of the pointwise
interval it is built from, which Section~\ref{sec:simsensitivity} shows is
governed by $K/n$; Supplement~\ref{app:calibration} derives a widening at
each pair's own ratio and reports it as a sensitivity.

Five contrasts on the primary log were fixed before the final analysis but
after earlier analyses of the same data, so their Holm-adjusted $p$-values
are descriptive (Supplement~\ref{sec:planned}); every other comparison in
this paper is exploratory and carries no multiplicity correction.

\subsection{The sensitivity decomposition}
\label{sec:sobol}

Because the declared surface is a complete factorial
(Section~\ref{sec:threesurfaces}), the variance of $V_s$ admits an exact,
orthogonal functional-ANOVA decomposition
\citep{sobol2001global,saltelli2010variance} under a product measure
$\mathbb{P} = \bigotimes_i w_i$, with first-order and total indices $S_i$ and
$S_{T_i}$ computed exactly rather than estimated
(Supplement~\ref{app:sobolest}); unlike the configuration-space
decomposition of \citet{hutter2014efficient}, the axes here are analysis
decisions and the components are exact rather than surrogate estimates.

\paragraph{The split is an error stratum, and the primary decomposition treats
it as one}
Five of the split axis's six levels are expanding-window folds on the same
data. They are different \emph{samples}, not different analyses, and a
decomposition that pools them reports sampling variability as specification
sensitivity. We call the stratum \emph{resampling} for brevity; because the
folds' training sets grow and their test periods move, it also carries the
time structure of the evaluation window --- the instability
\citet{giacomini2010unstable} and \citet{rossi2012window} address ---
and Section~\ref{sec:axes2} separates the split's main effect from its
interactions. Because the decomposition is exact and orthogonal, the repair is
a partition rather than a model: every component either involves the split or
does not, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strata}
1 = \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{\emptyset \neq u \subseteq \mathcal{A}} S_u}_{\text{analyst choice}}
+ \underbrace{S_{\{\text{quality}\}}}_{\text{counterfactual}}
+ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{u \ni \text{quality},\, u \cap \mathcal{A} \neq \emptyset,\, u \not\ni \text{split}} S_u}_{\text{mixed}}
+ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{u \ni \text{split}} S_u}_{\text{resampling}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{pipeline}, \text{baseline}\}$, the axes are
$\mathcal{A}$, the register-quality condition (which alters the state of the
world rather than the analysis) and the split, and the third term collects
the split-free components that join the register-quality condition to an
analyst axis; the four terms are computed exactly from the point estimates
(Table~\ref{tab:strata}) --- exact as arithmetic, though the estimates they
partition carry sampling error --- and the identity holds to \stratumPartitionError.
The first three survive fold-averaging and are decomposed again on the
fold-averaged surface, where the higher-order share is net of resampling.
\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:axes2} reports that decomposition as the primary
one}, and the pooled one beside it.

The share carried by interactions is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inttotal}
S_{\mathrm{int}} \;=\; 1 - \sum_i S_i ,
\end{equation}
which is not the largest per-axis involvement $\max_i (S_{T_i} - S_i)$,
because a two-way component appears in both its axes' total effects
(Supplement~\ref{app:measures}).

\subsection{Resolution regions and the robustness index}
\label{sec:regionsdef}

\begin{definition}[Cell labels and surface regions]
\label{def:regions}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the declared inference family of
Section~\ref{sec:threesurfaces} for a pair --- the cells that carry bootstrap
draws. Under the simultaneous band of Section~\ref{sec:simbands}, a
cell of
$\mathcal{F}$ is \emph{beneficial} if its lower band is above zero,
\emph{harmful} if its upper band is below zero, and \emph{unresolved}
otherwise. A surface is \emph{uniformly beneficial} if every cell of
$\mathcal{F}$ is beneficial; \emph{conditionally beneficial} if some are and
none is harmful;
\emph{uniformly harmful} and \emph{conditionally harmful} in mirror image;
\emph{sign-changing} if there is at least one cell of each kind; and
\emph{unresolved} if none is resolved --- \textbf{or if fewer than
\minResolvedSharePct\ of $\mathcal{F}$ is resolved at all}. The
\emph{robustness index} is
\[
\rho \;=\; \frac{\#\{\text{beneficial}\} - \#\{\text{harmful}\}}
{|\mathcal{F}|} \;\in\; [-1, 1],
\]
and every directional label is reported with the counts of beneficial,
harmful and unresolved cells it rests on.
\end{definition}

\noindent Here a cell is \emph{resolved}, and a region a \emph{resolution}
region, when the band determines a sign; the word is unrelated to the
resolution component of the Brier score \citep{murphy1973vector}.

\textbf{The minimum resolved share is a declared convention.}
Without it a surface earns a direction from as few as two resolved cells of a
hundred and eighty, and $\rho$ alone cannot distinguish two resolved cells
from ninety, so the counts are printed beside it. The declared threshold of
\minResolvedSharePct\ withdraws the direction on \nLabelsLostToMinShare\ of
the \nDirectionalLabels\ pairs that carry one, against \nLabelsLostMinOne\ at
\minShareLowPct\ and \nLabelsLostMinTen\ at \minShareHighPct. Because the
inference family is a subset of the admissible surface, a label computed on it
is cleaner than one computed on every cell, and \emph{uniformly beneficial}
gains the most; Section~\ref{sec:regions} therefore reports, for the one pair
that attains it, the share of its admissible cells the label covers.

Before the minimum-share rule is applied, \nCondHarmful\ surfaces
are conditionally harmful and \nUniformlyHarmful\ uniformly harmful; each
conditionally harmful label rests on at most \nCondHarmfulResolvedMax\
resolved cells of \nHelpdeskFamily, so the rule withdraws each, and
\nCondHarmfulMinShare\ harmful labels of either kind remain
(Table~\ref{tab:triple}). $\rho = 1$ is the only state in which one positive
number is a safe summary of a surface. The region is defined over the
\emph{scalar} family; the decision curve gets its own region
(Section~\ref{sec:dca}), so that a single extreme threshold cannot decide the
label of a whole surface. The intercept-only baseline is fitted but excluded
from every admissible set, because an increment measured against a model with
no features is inflated by construction.

\emph{Specification regret} --- what choosing one specification
costs against the best one --- is computed from the same surface but reported
only as a robustness check, because on this corpus it returns a null
(Supplement~\ref{sec:regretdef}; its reading in net benefit is in
Section~\ref{sec:nbregret}).

\section{The pre-specified benchmark design}
\label{sec:design}

Which datasets enter, which recorded attribute plays the predictor, which the
forecast target and which the baseline were fixed by written rules before any
result on a new log existed.

\subsection{What was fixed, and when}

The admission rules and targets were fixed in a dated protocol before any
result on a new log existed, and six later amendments are logged with their causes
and the results they changed (Supplement~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{This is
not a timestamped third-party registration}: the protocol is a file in a
repository whose history is under the analyst's own control, so a claim that
a rule was fixed in advance is no stronger than that repository. What the
design buys is that the rules are legible, dated and amendable only on the
record.

\subsection{Roles}

A log is admitted if the pre-specified rules can find three things in it
without reading an outcome.

\begin{description}
\item[The register $f$.] A high-cardinality attribute that is not
resource-like, has at least \minCardF\ distinct values, is reused across at
least \minReuseF\ cases on average, and --- by amendment~1 --- whose values
recur across the temporal split, so that a document number is not mistaken for
a maintained entity.
\item[The free field $g$.] A resource-like attribute (on the case-study log,
the assignment group), present on at least \minGPresent\ of cases, whose
value varies within a case. Only the \emph{first} value it takes on a case is
used as a feature, while the handover target is defined from the whole
sequence of values --- so the feature is what is known when the case opens,
the target is what happens afterwards, and the field cannot inform the model
about its own future.
\item[The intake block $B_0$.] Every remaining attribute of cardinality at
most \maxCardBZero\ that is neither an outcome, nor a timestamp, nor a
relabelling of the activity, nor a deterministic coarsening of $f$.
\end{description}

The nested baselines are built from these roles: the intercept-only model;
half of the intake block (its lower-cardinality half, fixed by a
deterministic order before any outcome is seen); the whole intake block; the
block plus the free field $g$; and, where a log admits a further field, that
field as a baseline of its own --- on the primary log the knowledge
reference, a pointer to a knowledge-base article. The intercept-only baseline
is in the surface and excluded from every admissible set
(Table~\ref{tab:axes}).

Table~\ref{tab:roles} gives, per pair, the attribute playing each role and the
number of levels each axis carries; Table~\ref{tab:master} repeats each
pair's admitted case count and register cardinality $K$. The \emph{target}
indexes surfaces rather than varying within one, and the per-pair factorial
is \nPerPairAxes\ axes wide, not the \nAxes\ of Definition~\ref{def:space}:
the forecast origin varies only on the case study's log, the operating point
only in the decision-curve family, and the cohort only in
Section~\ref{sec:cohort} (Section~\ref{sec:lim}).

\subsection{Targets, splits and exclusions}
\label{sec:targets}

Two targets are defined for every log --- \emph{handover}, whether more than
one resource group touches the case, and \emph{duration}, whether it runs
longer than the training half's median. Cases are ordered by first timestamp
and split $70/30$, with \nFolds\ expanding-window folds beside it, and fields
determined only at closure are excluded by name
\citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage,weytjens2022leakage}. A pair is
excluded only for a pre-specified reason, and every admitted pair is
analysed and reported --- running the surface only where the reduction
resolves would condition the analysis on the outcome.
Supplement~\ref{app:targets} gives the rules and the exclusion codes, and
Supplement~\ref{app:margin} the admission margin. The case-study log's
handover pair sits at the upper edge of the prevalence window, at
\prevAdmittedMax\ against a window that closes at \prevHi, and carries
\nCellsBpicFourteenHandover\ admissible cells, so every headline is also
reported on the IT service management (ITSM) family
(Supplement~\ref{sec:family}). The corpus is \nLogs\ public logs spanning
\nDomains\ domains: a \emph{benchmark of specification sensitivity across
heterogeneous forecasting problems}, not a replication of one finding, so no
effect magnitude is averaged across domains
(Supplement~\ref{app:construct}).

\subsection{What one number costs}
\label{sec:results-regret}

A conventional report disagrees in \emph{sign} with a median
\misreportEqualPct\ of the other admissible specifications under the
equal-level measure. This rate overstates the risk of a one-number report,
for three reasons that we apply cumulatively: not every axis is an analyst
choice, most cells are not resolved, and many disagreements are too small to
matter.

\textbf{The axes are not all the same kind of thing.}
Table~\ref{tab:axiskind} sums first-order indices into \emph{analyst
latitude} (the pipeline and the baseline), \emph{resampling} (the split) and
the \emph{counterfactual} (the register-quality condition), with the
higher-order remainder a fourth column. Within each metric, analyst latitude
carries a median \shareLatitudePct\ of the variance, resampling
\shareResamplingPct\ and the counterfactual \shareCounterfactualPct, with
resampling the larger on \nPairsResamplingOverLatitude\ of the \nPairs\
pairs; on a common headroom scale the ordering reverses, so neither ordering
is a corpus fact independent of the scale (Supplement~\ref{app:decompmeasure}).
Restricted to the analyst-latitude cells --- register clean, split at the
single holdout, a median \nCellsLatitude\ cells per pair --- the median
disagreement rate is \misreportLatitudePct, and that is what a one-number
report exposes a reader to. Across \nRefSensVariants\ choices of the cell
called conventional, the all-cells rate runs from \refSensMinPct\ to
\refSensMaxPct\ (Table~\ref{tab:refsens}).

\textbf{And most cells are not cells whose sign the data determine.} The
median pair resolves \nResolvedMedian\ cells of the \nCellsPerPairMedian\ it
carries, and sign agreement between two draws from a distribution centred
near zero is one half by construction. \textbf{And a sign flip between two
increments that both round to zero is not a misstatement anybody would act
on}: \nRefBelowMpid\ of the \nPairs\ reference increments are themselves
smaller than \mpidAuc\ AUC in absolute value, so a \emph{minimal practically important
difference} is declared --- $\mathrm{MPID} = \mpidAuc$ AUC --- and a
disagreement is counted only when the reference increment and the cell's own
increment both exceed it, on the AUC sub-surface, because Remark~\ref{prop:nonid}
forbids carrying an AUC threshold to another metric
(Table~\ref{tab:mpid}).

\textbf{On the cells the corpus can resolve, the rate is
\misreportPooledResolvedPct, and it is not an AUC statement.} Of the
\nResolvedCorpus\ cells whose whole-surface band excludes
zero, \nDisagreeResolved\ disagree in sign with the reference cell. Split by
metric that rate runs \misreportResolvedInstrumentMinPct\ on
\instrumentLeastDisagreement\ to \misreportResolvedInstrumentMaxPct\ on
\instrumentMostDisagreement: the pooled figure is carried by the metrics
that are not the one the reference cell is read in, which is
Remark~\ref{prop:nonid} showing up in the corpus. Read against the reference
cell of each cell's own metric instead, \nDisagreeOwnMetric\ of the
\nResolvedCorpus\ cells disagree, \misreportOwnMetricPct; all
\nLeaveBpicNineteenProper\ cells that leave the count are BPIC19's Brier,
log-loss and Nagelkerke cells, on a pair whose resolved AUC cells are beneficial and
whose resolved Brier-skill cells are harmful
(Supplement~\ref{app:ijf-samemetric}).

\textbf{Imposing all three restrictions leaves \nCellsFullyRestricted\ cells,
of which \nDisagreeFullyRestricted\ disagree.} Restrict, on the AUC
sub-surface, to the axes an analyst chooses, to the cells the band resolves,
and to increments that clear the MPID at both ends. Only the
\nInferenceCells\ of the \nCellsAuc\ admissible AUC cells that carry
bootstrap draws can be restricted this way; \nCellsFullyRestricted\ survive,
and the rate is \misreportFullyRestrictedPct. \textbf{We therefore do not
claim that a one-number report misstates a sign at a rate worth quoting as a
headline.} Two corollaries follow, and neither is comfortable for the
conventional report. First, few cells remain because \emph{the data resolve
so little}: \nCellsResolvedAuc\ of the \nInferenceCells\ banded AUC cells
resolve, \shareResolvedAucPct\ of them, and \nRefUnresolvedCell\ of the
\nPairs\ pairs do not resolve the reference cell itself. Second, the surface
still moves, by a distance that dwarfs the MPID: over the cells the band
resolves, the mean distance from the reference increment is a median
\madResolvedMedian\ AUC over the \nPairsMadResolved\ pairs that resolve any AUC
cell, and \madFullyRestrictedMedian\ under the full restriction. The
one-number report is seldom wrong about the sign; what it does not show is
how far the rest of the declared surface lies from it.

\subsection{Comparison with specification-curve analysis}
\label{sec:sca}

Specification-curve analysis \citep{simonsohn2020specification} enumerates
the reasonable specifications, plots the curve, and tests it against a sharp
null by permutation. Run unchanged on every admitted pair
(Supplement~\ref{app:sca}), it parts company with the region label on
\nScaDisagree\ of the \nScaPairs\ pairs: the permutation test rejects the
sharp null and the same cells contain increments of \emph{both} signs. The
permutation test answers \emph{is there anything here}; the region answers
\emph{may I quote a sign}, which is what a forecast user deciding whether to
act needs, and the two should be reported together.

\section{Decision-analytic evaluation}
\label{sec:dca}

An increment in AUC is not a quantity a service desk can act on. This section
reads the same surface in net benefit --- the decision-based value of a
forecast in the sense of \citet{murphy1993good} and
\citet{granger2000economic} --- which is the number of correctly nominated
cases per thousand arrivals, net of the false nominations, at a declared
exchange rate between the two.

\subsection{The operating point is a cost ratio, and calibration comes first}
\label{sec:calibfirst}

At threshold $\theta$ the net benefit of a rule that nominates when
$\hat p \geq \theta$ is
\[
\mathrm{NB}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{TP}(\theta)}{n}
\;-\; \frac{\mathrm{FP}(\theta)}{n}\cdot\frac{\theta}{1-\theta},
\]
so $(1-\theta)/\theta$ is the number of false nominations the desk accepts
for one true one, and choosing $\theta$ is choosing an operational cost ratio
that belongs to the analyst's declaration rather than to a grid search. We
report the whole grid --- \nGrid\ points from \gridLo\ to \gridHi\ --- and
name three points a desk might occupy: $\theta = \thetaCheap$ (\ratioCheap\
false nominations per true one, a cheap review), $\thetaBase$ (\ratioBase, a
review and a miss costing about the same) and $\thetaDear$ (\ratioDear,
scarce review capacity); a reader whose desk sits elsewhere reads their own
point off Figure~\ref{fig:dca}. A threshold is a statement about
probability, so every decision curve here is computed on \emph{calibrated}
scores, with the calibrator fitted inside the training half and rebuilt
inside every bootstrap refit, and a pre-specified rule excludes an arm whose
calibration slope leaves \calSlopeWindow\ rather than repairing it, because
recalibration is already a level of the pipeline axis
(Supplement~\ref{app:dcadetail}).

\subsection{Simultaneous bands change what can be said}
\label{sec:dcabands}

The decision-curve family is declared separately from the scalar surface and
carries its own max\nobreakdash-$t$ critical value, because net benefit and a scalar
metric are not the same scale. Its critical value is the empirical
quantile of Section~\ref{sec:simbands}, taken over the \emph{admissible}
family: a cell whose net-benefit difference is exactly zero in at least nine
draws of ten is zero by arithmetic, not by estimation, and is excluded before
the quantile is taken because it is not a statement about the register while
its vanishing spread inflates the value every other cell is judged by
(Section~\ref{sec:simband}). On the case-study family that rule excludes
\nDcaCellsExcludedDegenerate\ of \nDcaFamily\ cells and gives a critical
value of \dcaQEmp\ [\dcaQEmpLo, \dcaQEmpHi], against \dcaQ\ from the
multiplier approximation. The empirical band is the better of the two but
remains about a percentage point short of nominal on its matched family,
which fits this corpus less closely than the surface families do
(Section~\ref{sec:simband}).

Across the \nDcGrid\ operating points of the case-study curve \textbf{the
pointwise interval declares the increment resolvably positive at
\nBeneficialPointwise\ of them and the simultaneous band at
\nBeneficialSimultaneousEmp}, the lowest threshold the second resolves
being $\theta = \thetaBeneficialEmpMin$; the harmful counts are
\nHarmfulPointwise\ against \nHarmfulSimultaneousEmp. All are built from the
same draws, so what differs is the family and the level it is bought at:
those points are the price of a statement about a \emph{range} of thresholds
rather than about each one separately. Supplement~\ref{app:dcabandsfull}
gives the counts under the multiplier, under the order-statistic upper end of
the critical value and with the excluded cells kept, none of which changes
the conclusion; Figure~\ref{fig:dca} draws the curve (Table~\ref{tab:dca} is
a score-threshold sensitivity analysis on raw scores, not this curve). The
pointwise interval is not widened by a family-wise factor, and no $(n,K)$
widening is applied to this family.

\subsection{The decision, in units a service desk uses}
\label{sec:nbregret}

The comparison is run in net benefit per thousand cases, over a declared
distribution of exchange rates a desk plausibly holds
(\deskQlo\ to \deskQhi, centred \deskQmid), and only on models that pass the
pre-specified calibration rule --- which excludes \nModelsDcaExcluded\ of
\nModelsDca\ models, a model being a pair--pipeline--baseline triple whose
two arms must both pass, and \nPairsDcaExcluded\ of \nPairs\ pairs.
This is the most consequential operational finding: a decision curve read off
a model whose probabilities are wrong is read at a threshold the model does
not have.

\textbf{The four decision rules separate on most pairs, with the excess
falling on the conservative rule rather than the one-number rule}
(Supplement~\ref{app:deskfull}). Larger than anything the choice of decision
rule costs a one-number report is which fields the forecast origin makes
admissible: on the case-study log's pre-specified handover target, the
register promises \promisedPerThousand\ per thousand against the arrival
attributes alone and delivers \deliveredPerThousand\ once the assignment
group and the knowledge reference --- assumed recorded by incident creation
--- are in the benchmark, a shortfall of \shortfallPerThousand. Supplement~\ref{app:deskfull} carries the rules, the
exchange-rate distribution and the exclusions; Tables~\ref{tab:desk}
and~\ref{tab:rules} the numbers.

\section{Simulation against a known answer}
\label{sec:sim}

Everything so far has been measured on real logs, where the answer is not
known. This section measures the interval and the band where it is, against
worlds whose population increment is computed in closed form. The full tables are in
Supplement~\ref{app:tables} and the diagnosis of the one world that failed is
in Supplement~\ref{app:noisy}.

\subsection{What the simulation certifies of the pointwise interval}
\label{sec:simworlds}
\label{sec:simcoverage}
\label{sec:simsensitivity}

The simulation uses \nWorlds\ worlds --- four of them misspecified for the
estimator --- at \nSimReps\ replicates each, with the covariate space finite
so that the population AUC of any score function is computed in closed form.
Two estimands are separated because under misspecification they differ:
$V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$, the increment between the two Bayes-optimal rules, and
$V_{\mathrm{limit}}$, the increment between the two fitted pipelines' limits
(the simulation resamples by subset moving blocks, whereas the intervals
and bands the article reports draw Dirichlet weights per block,
Section~\ref{sec:boot}, so what is certified here is the subset
construction, and Supplement~\ref{app:schemes} compares the two schemes on
the corpus's own design);
a resampling interval can only cover the second (Supplement~\ref{app:worlds},
Table~\ref{tab:sim31}). Coverage estimates carry a Monte Carlo standard error
of about $\simCoverageSE$ percentage points, which is the precision every
comparison here should be read at; Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} draws them by
construction and world.

Three things are established and one is not. The nested basic interval
covers a median \coverageNestedBasicMedian\ against the fixed-model
percentile interval's \coverageNaiveMedian, and on the sparse world the
\emph{percentile} interval collapses to \coverageSparsePct\ while the basic
construction restores it to \coverageSparseBasic, which is why every interval
and band here is the basic one (Supplement~\ref{app:refit}). Against
$V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ every construction undercovers on the drifting world
--- \coverageDriftOracle\ at best --- and it should, because when the
coefficients move between eras the estimator is not consistent for the
oracle quantity, and no resampling scheme repairs that. Varying sample size
and register cardinality jointly on a grid of \nSimGridCells\ $(n, K)$
cells, up to \simNMax\ training rows and $K = \simKMax$ levels
(Table~\ref{tab:sim31grid}), the ratio $K/n$ governs the basic interval's
coverage --- a median \coverageGridMedian\ and a minimum \coverageGridMin\
across that grid, short
of nominal everywhere --- and does not exhaust it: holding the register at
one cardinality while the training half grows from \simGridFixedNa\ to
\simGridFixedNb\ rows moves coverage from \simGridFixedCovA\ to
\simGridFixedCovB\ (Supplement~\ref{app:calibration}). This corpus sits in
the regime where that matters, its median pair at $K/n = \corpusRatioMedian$
with \nPairsRatioAboveTenth\ of \nPairs\ pairs above a tenth, so a nominal
pointwise interval on them is narrower than it should be; past a crossing in
$n$ the basic interval stops containing its own point estimate
(Supplement~\ref{app:planereach}). The block length, varied over
\nSimBlockLengths\ settings around $\lceil n^{1/3} \rceil$, moves coverage by
up to \coverageBlockSpreadMaxPts\ within a world without changing any
comparison the paper rests on.

\subsection{The band's family-wise coverage}
\label{sec:simband}

Everything above validates a \emph{pointwise} interval. Two of this paper's
three reporting objects are functions of a \emph{simultaneous} band, and this
section measures what the band's critical value covers.

\textbf{The design, and why its answer is an upper bound.} One draw from the
sampling distribution of a synthetic family gives the point estimate, and as
many further draws from that same distribution as the matched corpus family
carries give the bootstrap, so the bootstrap is the sampling distribution
itself: every way a resampling scheme can be wrong is switched off, and a
coverage measured here is the most the real procedure can attain. The
families are the ones the corpus runs --- every pair's surface family of
\nCellsPerPairMedian\ cells at \nDrawsSurface\ draws, the corpus's modal
decision-curve family and the case study's own curve family
(Table~\ref{tab:bandcov}) --- matched in cross-cell correlation, effective
rank, per-cell excess kurtosis and the ratio of the empirical quantile to the
multiplier on the corpus's own families. The surface family reproduces that
ratio at \ratioSynthWhole\ against the corpus's median \ratioCorpusWhole,
inside its interquartile range; the curve family reaches \ratioSynthDca\
against \ratioCorpusDca, outside it, so its numbers below transfer less well.
Four regimes are run: Gaussian draws, the case the multiplier is derived for;
draws carrying this corpus's tails; the same with the degenerate cells
described below; and a fixed heterogeneous \emph{non-zero} truth, under which
a cell can be resolved correctly and family-wise coverage is the quantity a
region label actually depends on. Every coverage here has a Monte Carlo
standard error of at most \bandCoverageSEMax\ (Supplement~\ref{app:degencells}).

\textbf{The empirical quantile attains its level on the matched family; the
multiplier does not, and no draw budget makes it.} On every pair's surface
family, under this corpus's tails, the empirical quantile of the observed
maxima covers \covEmpHeavyWholeAtDesign\ against a nominal $95\%$, at a
Monte Carlo standard error of \covEmpHeavyWholeAtDesignSE, and
\covEmpNonzeroWholeAtDesign\ under the non-zero truth; the order-statistic
upper end of the same quantile covers \covEmpHiHeavyWholeAtDesign. The
Gaussian multiplier covers \covMultHeavyWholeAtDesign\ and
\covMultNonzeroWholeAtDesign\ on the same families --- four to six standard
errors short --- and the two change places under the Gaussian draws it
is derived for (Supplement~\ref{app:twoq}). Across draw counts at the surface
family the multiplier rises steeply out of the smallest budgets and then
\emph{plateaus}: \covMultBootThirtyThree\ at \nBootThirtyThree\ draws,
\covMultBootOneFifty\ at \nBootOneFifty, \covMultBootFourHundred\ at
\nBootFourHundred\ and \covMultBootOneThousand\ at \nBootOneThousand. The
empirical quantile behaves differently over the same counts ---
\covEmpBootThirtyThree, \covEmpBootOneFifty, \covEmpBootFourHundred\ and
\covEmpBootOneThousand\ --- because an order statistic of \nBootThirtyThree\
maxima is badly determined and one of \nDrawsSurface\ is not, which is why
the construction the paper reports is available at this corpus's draw count
and would not have been at a third of it. What the level costs is width: the
empirical band is \widthEmpOverMultAtDesign\ times as wide as the
multiplier's (the mean band-width ratio of this run), and
Section~\ref{sec:regions} prints what each resolves.

\textbf{The decision-curve families, and the cells that are zero by
arithmetic.} Where both arms treat every case alike the net-benefit
difference is zero by arithmetic; \nDegenerateCellsDca\ cells of the
corpus's curve families are exactly zero in at least nine draws of ten, and
standardising such a cell by its vanishing spread inflates the critical value
every other cell is judged by, so a declared rule removes such cells from a
decision-curve family before the quantile is taken
(Supplement~\ref{app:degencells}). On the modal curve family so treated the
empirical quantile covers \covEmpHeavyDcaAtDesign\ under the corpus's tails
against the multiplier's \covMultHeavyDcaAtDesign, and on the case study's
own family \covEmpHeavyCaseAtDesign\ against \covMultHeavyCaseAtDesign.
\textbf{On the curve families the empirical quantile is therefore about a
point short of its level}, on a synthetic family that under-reproduces the
corpus's estimator ratio, and it is reported as the better of the two
estimators rather than as one measured at nominal.

\paragraph{What the simulation does not establish}
It certifies the pointwise constructions on \nWorlds\ worlds, a grid of
\nSimGridCells\ $(n, K)$ cells and \nSimBlockLengths\ block lengths, and
measures the band's critical value on the \nBandCoverageFamilies\ matched
families of Section~\ref{sec:simband} under four regimes --- on nothing else.
The mixing the moving-block bootstrap needs is assumed and not tested, and
the stationarity it also needs is tested (Section~\ref{sec:estimand}) and does
not hold on every pair; the split point is fixed within a draw, so
split-position uncertainty is outside every interval reported here; and the
drifting world shows what happens when the estimator is not consistent for
the quantity a reader cares about. The $(n, K)$ widening that addresses the
pointwise channel is fitted pointwise, applied simultaneously and measurably
misspecified in $n$, and is reported as a sensitivity only
(Supplement~\ref{app:calibration}).

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:lim}

\paragraph{Construct --- this is forecast performance, not value}
Nothing here measures what a register costs to acquire or maintain, whether
acting on a forecast changes an outcome, or what a data programme is worth.
The corpus is a family of related questions rather than replications --- the
two pre-specified targets label the same case identically on a median
\targetAgreement\ of cases, and the registers run from a configuration item to
a legal article --- so every corpus statistic is about \emph{sensitivity},
every one is reported on the \nItsmPairs\ ITSM pairs as well
(Table~\ref{tab:family}), and Table~\ref{tab:construct} names each pair's own
construct threat. The pre-specified handover target on the case study's log is
close to degenerate at prevalence \prevAdmittedMax\ and is where
Section~\ref{sec:cohort} shows the register's sign turns; whether a generic
handover rule is a meaningful target on ITSM logs and whether the prevalence
window should close below \prevHi\ are open, and the rule was not narrowed
after seeing which pair it admits. The quality mechanisms are applied to both
halves with parameters estimated from training alone
(Section~\ref{sec:quality}), so they are the operational counterfactual and
not a train--test mismatch, but they remain constructed, and validating them
against a genuinely incomplete register is the obvious next study.

\paragraph{Internal --- what the band's coverage does and does not establish}
Section~\ref{sec:simband} measures family-wise coverage over matched families
whose bootstrap is \emph{ideal}, so every coverage there is an upper bound on
what the procedure attains, and the empirical critical value the region
labels rest on attains its level there while the multiplier approximation
does not. Three qualifications are limits rather than results --- the
$K/n$-governed undercoverage of the pointwise interval underneath the band,
the width at which the level is bought, and a decision-curve critical value
about a point short of its level --- and Supplement~\ref{app:threats} states
them, with the mechanisms this evidence rules out and the further
assumptions it names.

\paragraph{Internal --- the estimand, and nested comparisons near zero}
Definition~\ref{def:estimand} defines the increment as a large-sample limit
of the fitted rule, while the split axis, the refitting bootstrap and the
forecast origins treat the forecasting \emph{method}, with its finite
estimation window, as the object, as \citet{giacomini2006conditional} do.
Near a zero increment the two differ: estimating the larger model's extra
parameters biases an out-of-sample comparison against it
\citep{clark2007approximately}, so some harmful labels on proper scores may
be finite-sample effects rather than evidence against the predictor. At the
reference cell a Clark--West comparison favours the predictor on
\nRejectCw\ of the \nPairs\ pairs against \nRejectDmBrier\ for the raw
Brier comparison, counting one-sided rejections in the predictor's favour at
the 5\% level, and \nCwReverses\ of those pairs have a negative raw Brier
difference (Table~\ref{tab:cwtests}). A surface-wide version of that comparison, a
zero-increment world in the simulation of Section~\ref{sec:sim}, and
bootstrap weights shared across splits --- which would let sampling noise be
subtracted from the split stratum --- each need a new bootstrap run and are
left open.

\paragraph{External --- the design space is declared, not complete}
$\mathcal{S}$ has \nAxes\ axes in Definition~\ref{def:space} and the per-pair
factorial varies \nPerPairAxes\ of them (Table~\ref{tab:roles}); the forecast
origin varies on the case study's log alone, the operating point only inside
the decision-curve family, the cohort only in the case study's cohort design,
and the target indexes surfaces rather than varying within one. It could have
more --- tuning protocol, missing-data treatment, target horizon, label-noise
model --- so every claim here is relative to this space. Prefix length,
prefix bucketing and sequence encoding are not axes, because this study
forecasts at one origin from creation-time attributes, so no result here
speaks to benchmarks that vary them. Forecast origins later in a case's life
are a finer grid of the forecast-origin axis, and a pilot on one log
(Supplement~\ref{app:prefix}) shows that they matter: holding the register
fixed and moving only the prefix, the increment falls from \VPrefixZero\ AUC
at case creation to \VPrefixTwo\ two events later and \VPrefixEight\ at the
longest prefix run, and is undetermined in sign at \nPrefixUnresolved\ of
\nPrefixLevels\ prefixes. Case creation --- the origin every other result here
occupies --- is thus the most favourable prefix for a creation-time register,
and two studies reporting from the two ends of that axis would differ by a
factor of \prefixDropFactor\ without either being wrong. One log, one register
and no sequence encoder is a pilot, and the omission stands.

\paragraph{External --- narrower limits}
The inference surface is smaller than the declared one and always will be,
because a draw refits every arm of every cell, so every region label is a
claim about a fraction of the space the point estimates cover, on which
\emph{uniformly beneficial} is easier to earn
(Section~\ref{sec:threesurfaces}), and every label stays conditional on the
register-quality axis being averaged over. The pipeline axis is crossed on the
\nItsmCrossed\ ITSM pairs and not elsewhere, and what the crossing found is
not a clean replication (Supplement~\ref{app:axes2full}), so the case study's
ordering is the common case and not a corpus fact. The case study is one
bank's service desk on one public log, with the availability question at its
centre settled by probability rather than by a timestamped field-value
history. And reporting practice is not measured (Supplement~\ref{app:pilot}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

What a predictor adds to a forecast is a functional of design choices, not a
number. This paper made that operational: a declared design space, a surface
over it, an exact decomposition of the surface's variance that separates the
choices an analyst makes from the resampling the design also contains,
simultaneous bands whose family-wise coverage is measured in a matched
simulation rather than assumed --- an upper bound, since that simulation's
bootstrap is ideal, and the pointwise intervals beneath the bands undercover
where the predictor has many levels relative to the sample --- and region
labels that say where a sign is determined and withhold a direction where too
little of the surface resolves to support one.

\textbf{On \nSpreadExceedsRealistic\ of \nPairs\ pairs the baseline alone
--- among the baselines an analyst would actually build --- moves the
increment further than the increment itself.}

\textbf{Most of a pooled specification surface is resampling, and net of it
different pairs turn on different analyst choices.} The components involving
the split carry a median \shareResamplingStratumPct\ of the variance, below
the \splitNullShareMaxPct\ a surface with no signal would put there. Net of
them the largest first-order index at the median pair is
\foldLargestFirstOrderPct\ against a higher-order share of
\foldInteractionTotalPct, and the interactions exceed the largest
within-metric first-order index on \nFoldInteractionExceeds\ of \nPairs\
pairs, against \nPooledInteractionExceeds\ before the resampling stratum is
removed, so once the folds are treated as resamples the interactions do not
dominate (Section~\ref{sec:axes2}). Which axis leads is a
property of the pair --- the baseline on \nFoldLeadPairsRung\ pairs, the
register-quality condition on \nFoldLeadPairsQuality\ and the pipeline on
\nFoldLeadPairsPipeline\ --- so an analyst cannot learn from somebody else's
paper which of their own choices their answer turns on.

\textbf{A one-number report is usually right about the sign and usually silent
about the size.} Once a sign has to be determined by the data \emph{and} worth
acting on at \mpidAuc\ AUC, the number of the \nCellsFullyRestricted\
\emph{AUC} cells surviving all three restrictions that contradict the
conventional report is \nDisagreeFullyRestricted{}, \textbf{so the
sign-disagreement rate is not a headline} (Section~\ref{sec:results-regret}).
What the conventional report does not say is that the cells the band
\emph{resolves}, on the \nPairsMadResolved\ pairs that resolve any, lie a
median \madResolvedMedian\ AUC away from the one it
stands on, and that on \nRefUnresolvedCell\ of \nPairs\ pairs the data do not
determine the sign of its own cell at all.

\textbf{The forecast origin is an argument of the estimand, not a preliminary.}
On the case study, moving the forecast from first touch to incident creation
moves the increment by \deltaCohortAtTau\ AUC through the population, by a
further \deltaSnapshotAtTau\ through the information once the population is
held fixed, and by \deltaPopulationResidual\ more through the incidents that
have no interaction record --- before any baseline changes; and the same
log's answer changes sign when the target changes, within the minimal
practically important difference, with \shareTargetPct\ of the difference on
the target and \shareCohortPct\ on the cohort. A desk that
adopted this register on its estimate against the arrival attributes alone,
and deployed it where the assignment group and the knowledge reference are
already recorded, would be promised \promisedPerThousand\ net true positives
per thousand cases on the pre-specified handover target and receive
\deliveredPerThousand.

\textbf{And a threshold is not always a cost ratio.} On \nModelsDcaExcluded\
of \nModelsDca\ models --- pair--pipeline--baseline triples --- the
calibration slope falls outside \calSlopeWindow, so the threshold at which a
decision curve is read cannot be interpreted as the cost ratio the
decision-analytic argument requires (Section~\ref{sec:calibfirst}).

\textbf{What to report.} The design space and the measure over it; which of
the three sets of specifications a claim quantifies over; the absolute
increment at every baseline rather than a ratio; the decomposition with the
resampling stratum separated and $1 - \sum_i S_i$ reported as such; the region
and $\rho$ under the band's own family, with the counts they rest on; the
forecast origin and the target as parts of the estimand; a minimal
practically important difference declared before any disagreement rate; and
the reduction $R$ last if at all, with a Fieller set and only where its
denominator is resolvably positive. Supplement~\ref{app:standard} states the
standard in full, with a six-step recipe, \pkgname\ \citep{fieldvalue} that
implements the objects, and a worked example on a dataset that is not an
event log.
