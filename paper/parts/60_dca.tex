
\section{Decision-analytic evaluation}
\label{sec:dca}

An increment in AUC is not a quantity a service desk can act on. This section
reads the same surface in net benefit, which is: the number of correctly
nominated cases per thousand arrivals, net of the false nominations, at a
declared exchange rate between the two.

\subsection{The operating point is a cost ratio, not a knob}

At threshold $\theta$ the net benefit of a rule that nominates when
$\hat p \geq \theta$ is
\[
\mathrm{NB}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{TP}(\theta)}{n}
\;-\; \frac{\mathrm{FP}(\theta)}{n}\cdot\frac{\theta}{1-\theta},
\]
so $\theta/(1-\theta)$ is the number of false nominations the desk is willing
to accept for one true one. Choosing $\theta$ is therefore choosing an
operational cost ratio, and it belongs to the analyst's declaration rather
than to a grid search. We report the whole grid --- \nGrid\ points from
\gridLo\ to \gridHi\ --- and name three points a desk might actually occupy:
\thetaCheap, where a review is cheap relative to a missed reassignment;
\thetaBase, the estate's own base rate; and \thetaDear, where review capacity
is scarce.

\subsection{What the operational statement does not say}

Net benefit converts a discrimination increment into units the desk
recognises. It does not convert it into money, and three gaps remain, each of
which would need data this study does not have.

First, nominating a case for review is not the same as preventing a
reassignment. Net benefit counts correct nominations; whether acting on one
changes the outcome is an interventional question and this is observational
evidence.

Second, the exchange rate is declared, not measured. A desk's true ratio of
review cost to reassignment cost is an organisational quantity; we report the
whole curve so that a reader who knows theirs can read off their own point,
and we do not pretend to know it.

Third, the cost of the register itself --- discovery tooling, reconciliation
effort, the staff who keep it current --- is nowhere in this evidence. A
register that is worth \VTtwoGroup\ AUC on one task may still be worth buying
for a dozen other reasons, and may not be worth buying for this one. Nothing
here settles that, and Section~\ref{sec:lim} says so again because it is the
inference a reader is most likely to draw and least entitled to.
