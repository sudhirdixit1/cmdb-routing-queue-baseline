\section{Estimation and inference}
\label{sec:inference}

This section gives the estimator of one cell and its interval, then the
objects that read the surface as a whole: the set a claim ranges over,
simultaneous bands, the variance decomposition, and the regions where a sign
is determined.

\subsection{The estimator, and uncertainty that includes the model}
\label{sec:boot}

The pipeline is frozen and identical on every log, and the \emph{pipeline}
axis spans encodings as well as model families; all models are fitted with
\texttt{scikit-learn} \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Attributes are assigned to roles by the
pre-specified rules of Section~\ref{sec:design}, cases are ordered by their
first timestamp, and the split is temporal (Supplement~\ref{app:pipeline}).
The usual interval for such an increment resamples test rows with the models
held fixed, which excludes training-sample, model-selection, encoder and
split variability and the temporal dependence between cases. Every interval
here is instead a \emph{nested moving-block bootstrap}: one draw resamples
the training half by moving blocks \citep{kunsch1989jackknife} of length
$\lceil n^{1/3} \rceil$, refits both \emph{arms} of every comparison --- the
model with the register and the nested model without it --- under every
baseline, learner and quality mechanism, and evaluates on a moving-block
resample of the test half, with encoders, frequency tables, target
encodings, register rankings and calibrators rebuilt inside the draw. The
nested construction is used because the variability it captures is part of
the estimand, not because it covers better: in the simulation of
Section~\ref{sec:sim} the two intervals cannot be told apart at its Monte
Carlo precision.

\noindent Throughout, $K$ denotes the \textbf{cardinality of the register
field} --- the number of distinct values $f$ takes on a pair, \cardFMedian\ at
the median pair and \cardFMax\ at the largest (Table~\ref{tab:master}) --- and
$n$ the number of training cases. The ratio $K/n$ governs everything in this
subsection and in Section~\ref{sec:regions}.

\paragraph{Refitting inside the draw breaks the percentile interval}
Refitting every arm inside every draw displaces the bootstrap distribution
from the estimate. Part of the cause is that a multinomial resample holds
only about $1 - e^{-1}$ of a high-cardinality categorical's distinct levels,
so a refit sees a sparser register, but removing that loss leaves most of
the displacement standing (next paragraph). A percentile interval taken from
a displaced distribution is a correct interval for the wrong quantity. On the sparse world of Section~\ref{sec:sim} it covers
\coverageSparsePct\ against a nominal $95\%$, \emph{worse} than the
fixed-model interval refitting was meant to improve on. Every interval here
is therefore the \emph{basic} (pivotal) interval
$[\,2\hat{V} - q_{1-\alpha/2},\; 2\hat{V} - q_{\alpha/2}\,]$
\citep{davison1997bootstrap}, and simultaneous bands are recentred by the
same shift (Supplement~\ref{app:pctfail}).

\paragraph{Weights rather than subsets}
Every band comes from a bootstrap that draws Dirichlet \emph{weights} per
moving block rather than a subset of rows, so every register level is present
in every refit and no cell loses its band because a draw dropped an arm ---
\nUnbandableCells\ unbandable cells under weights, against
\nUnbandableSameDesign\ of \nUnbandableSameDesignOf\ under a multinomial
resample of the same design. Weights do not remove the displacement of the
bootstrap distribution; its residual cause is not identified here and is
listed among the limitations (Supplement~\ref{app:schemes} and
Supplement~\ref{app:threats}). One candidate mechanism, not established
here, is that block weights lower the effective training size, so a draw sits
lower on the learning curve that $K/n$ governs than the full-sample estimate
does.

The analysis code, and separate code that re-derives every reported number,
were written with the help of a generative-AI tool (see the declaration
before the references); no reported number depends on the tool being run
again, because the archived code regenerates every result from the public
logs (see the data availability statement).

\subsection{Three surfaces, and which one a claim may quantify over}
\label{sec:threesurfaces}

A statement of the form \emph{this surface is uniformly beneficial} ranges
over a set of specifications, and three sets are distinguished and counted
(Table~\ref{tab:denominator}). The \textbf{declared surface} is every
admissible specification: the product of the axis levels declared in
Section~\ref{sec:design}. The \textbf{computational surface} is every
specification actually evaluated; here it equals the declared surface: all
\nDeclaredAdmissibleScalar\ declared scalar cells were evaluated, with
\nMissingCells\ missing and \nDuplicateCells\ duplicated. The
\textbf{inference surface} is every specification carrying bootstrap draws;
it is smaller because a draw refits the whole pipeline, so its cost is the
declared surface's multiplied by the draw count.

The rule that selects the inference surface reads neither a result nor the
log: one design on every pair --- two learners, two splits, three
register-quality conditions and three admissible baselines, crossed,
\nCellsPerDecomposition\ cells a pair per metric and \nInferenceScalar\
scalar family members across the corpus, at \nDrawsSurface\ draws --- a
complete factorial, identical on every pair (Supplement~\ref{app:pipeline};
Supplement~\ref{app:infanova} compares the decomposition on the two
surfaces). The inference family is a median
\inferenceShareAdmissibleMedianPct\ of a pair's admissible cells, and less
than a twentieth on the pair with the richest grid; \textbf{a label
computed over fewer cells is systematically cleaner than one computed over
all of them} (Section~\ref{sec:regionsdef}). Only the region labels use the
inference surface; the decomposition, the sign-disagreement rate (the share
of cells whose sign differs from the conventional report's) and the regret
comparison use the full declared surface.

\subsection{Simultaneous bands over the family the claim ranges over}
\label{sec:simbands}

A decision curve read across \nGrid\ thresholds, a set of nested baselines
read together, or a surface read across every admissible cell, is a family of
statements, and a pointwise interval does not control the probability that
\emph{some} member of the family is wrong. For a declared family
$\mathcal{F}$ of cells with bootstrap draws $V^{(b)}_c$ and per-cell standard
errors $\hat\sigma_c$, let
\[
T^{(b)} \;=\; \max_{c \in \mathcal{F}}
\frac{\bigl|V^{(b)}_c - \bar{V}_c\bigr|}{\hat\sigma_c},
\qquad
q_{1-\alpha} = \text{the } (1-\alpha)\text{ quantile of } \{T^{(b)}\},
\]
and report $\hat{V}_c \pm q_{1-\alpha}\hat\sigma_c$, recentred by the basic
construction's shift as above. This is the max-$t$
(Romano--Wolf) construction \citep{romano2005stepdown} and has family-wise
coverage over $\mathcal{F}$ asymptotically, and over nothing larger. It
differs from the model confidence set \citep{hansen2011mcs} in what it
returns: the confidence set is the subset of a family not rejected as
inferior to its best member, a statement about \emph{which} specifications
survive; the band is a simultaneous interval for \emph{every} member's
increment, from which a region label reads where the sign is determined and
a robustness index how much of the family agrees. Both rest on a bootstrap
of the maximum over the family; the objects read off it are different.

\paragraph{Which family a band must cover}
Within-cell bands, each covering the \nInstruments\ metrics of one cell, do
not cover the several hundred cells that a surface-level label ranges over.
The \textsc{whole-surface} family --- max-$t$ over \emph{every} scalar cell
of the pair's inference family --- is the only family a surface-level claim
may use; it has \familySizeMedian\ members on every pair against the within-cell
family's \nInstruments, at a median critical value of \maxTSurfaceMedian\
against that family's \maxTWithinMedian\ and the pointwise $1.96$. Family
control is per log--target pair, so every cross-pair statement here is
descriptive; Supplement~\ref{app:families} defines the four interval objects
and the rule for dropping a replicate that leaves a cell without outcome
variation.

\paragraph{Estimating the critical value}
Two estimators of $q_{1-\alpha}$ are computed on every family. The first is
the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of the $B$ observed maxima --- the
Romano--Wolf construction as cited --- whose precision is that of an order
statistic of $B$ values. The second multiplies the standardised draws by
independent standard normals \citep{chernozhukov2013gaussian}, which frees
the precision of $q$ from $B$ but reproduces only the excursions a Gaussian
process makes; on every family here it lies below the empirical quantile's
whole order-statistic interval (Supplement~\ref{app:twoq}). \textbf{The
operative critical value is the empirical quantile}; the multiplier's median
on the whole-surface families is \maxTSurfaceMultMedian, and
Section~\ref{sec:simband} measures the family-wise coverage of both against a
known answer. A max-$t$ band also inherits the coverage of the pointwise
interval it is built from, which Section~\ref{sec:simsensitivity} shows is
governed by $K/n$; Supplement~\ref{app:calibration} derives a widening at
each pair's own ratio and reports it as a sensitivity.

Five contrasts on the primary log were fixed before the final analysis but
after earlier analyses of the same data, so their Holm-adjusted $p$-values
are descriptive (Supplement~\ref{sec:planned}); every other comparison in
this paper is exploratory and carries no multiplicity correction.

\subsection{The sensitivity decomposition}
\label{sec:sobol}

Because the declared surface is a complete factorial
(Section~\ref{sec:threesurfaces}), the variance of $V_s$ admits an exact,
orthogonal functional-ANOVA decomposition
\citep{sobol2001global,saltelli2010variance} under a product measure
$\mathbb{P} = \bigotimes_i w_i$, with first-order and total indices $S_i$ and
$S_{T_i}$ computed exactly rather than estimated
(Supplement~\ref{app:sobolest}); unlike the configuration-space
decomposition of \citet{hutter2014efficient}, the axes here are analysis
decisions and the components are exact rather than surrogate estimates.

\paragraph{The split is an error stratum, and the primary decomposition treats
it as one}
Five of the split axis's six levels are expanding-window folds on the same
data. They are different \emph{samples}, not different analyses, and a
decomposition that pools them reports sampling variability as specification
sensitivity. We call the stratum \emph{resampling} for brevity; because the
folds' training sets grow and their test periods move, it also carries the
time structure of the evaluation window --- the instability
\citet{giacomini2010unstable} and \citet{rossi2012window} address ---
and Section~\ref{sec:axes2} separates the split's main effect from its
interactions. Because the decomposition is exact and orthogonal, the repair is
a partition rather than a model: every component either involves the split or
does not, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strata}
1 = \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{\emptyset \neq u \subseteq \mathcal{A}} S_u}_{\text{analyst choice}}
+ \underbrace{S_{\{\text{quality}\}}}_{\text{counterfactual}}
+ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{u \ni \text{quality},\, u \cap \mathcal{A} \neq \emptyset,\, u \not\ni \text{split}} S_u}_{\text{mixed}}
+ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{u \ni \text{split}} S_u}_{\text{resampling}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{pipeline}, \text{baseline}\}$, the axes are
$\mathcal{A}$, the register-quality condition (which alters the state of the
world rather than the analysis) and the split, and the third term collects
the split-free components that join the register-quality condition to an
analyst axis; the four terms are computed exactly from the point estimates
(Table~\ref{tab:strata}) --- exact as arithmetic, though the estimates they
partition carry sampling error --- and the identity holds to \stratumPartitionError.
The first three survive fold-averaging and are decomposed again on the
fold-averaged surface, where the higher-order share is net of resampling.
\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:axes2} reports that decomposition as the primary
one}, and the pooled one beside it.

The share carried by interactions is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inttotal}
S_{\mathrm{int}} \;=\; 1 - \sum_i S_i ,
\end{equation}
which is not the largest per-axis involvement $\max_i (S_{T_i} - S_i)$,
because a two-way component appears in both its axes' total effects
(Supplement~\ref{app:measures}).

\subsection{Resolution regions and the robustness index}
\label{sec:regionsdef}

\begin{definition}[Cell labels and surface regions]
\label{def:regions}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the declared inference family of
Section~\ref{sec:threesurfaces} for a pair --- the cells that carry bootstrap
draws. Under the simultaneous band of Section~\ref{sec:simbands}, a
cell of
$\mathcal{F}$ is \emph{beneficial} if its lower band is above zero,
\emph{harmful} if its upper band is below zero, and \emph{unresolved}
otherwise. A surface is \emph{uniformly beneficial} if every cell of
$\mathcal{F}$ is beneficial; \emph{conditionally beneficial} if some are and
none is harmful;
\emph{uniformly harmful} and \emph{conditionally harmful} in mirror image;
\emph{sign-changing} if there is at least one cell of each kind; and
\emph{unresolved} if none is resolved --- \textbf{or if fewer than
\minResolvedSharePct\ of $\mathcal{F}$ is resolved at all}. The
\emph{robustness index} is
\[
\rho \;=\; \frac{\#\{\text{beneficial}\} - \#\{\text{harmful}\}}
{|\mathcal{F}|} \;\in\; [-1, 1],
\]
and every directional label is reported with the counts of beneficial,
harmful and unresolved cells it rests on.
\end{definition}

\noindent Here a cell is \emph{resolved}, and a region a \emph{resolution}
region, when the band determines a sign; the word is unrelated to the
resolution component of the Brier score \citep{murphy1973vector}.

\textbf{The minimum resolved share is a declared convention.}
Without it a surface earns a direction from as few as two resolved cells of a
hundred and eighty, and $\rho$ alone cannot distinguish two resolved cells
from ninety, so the counts are printed beside it. The declared threshold of
\minResolvedSharePct\ withdraws the direction on \nLabelsLostToMinShare\ of
the \nDirectionalLabels\ pairs that carry one, against \nLabelsLostMinOne\ at
\minShareLowPct\ and \nLabelsLostMinTen\ at \minShareHighPct. Because the
inference family is a subset of the admissible surface, a label computed on it
is cleaner than one computed on every cell, and \emph{uniformly beneficial}
gains the most; Section~\ref{sec:regions} therefore reports, for the one pair
that attains it, the share of its admissible cells the label covers.

Before the minimum-share rule is applied, \nCondHarmful\ surfaces
are conditionally harmful and \nUniformlyHarmful\ uniformly harmful; each
conditionally harmful label rests on at most \nCondHarmfulResolvedMax\
resolved cells of \nHelpdeskFamily, so the rule withdraws each, and
\nCondHarmfulMinShare\ harmful labels of either kind remain
(Table~\ref{tab:triple}). $\rho = 1$ is the only state in which one positive
number is a safe summary of a surface. The region is defined over the
\emph{scalar} family; the decision curve gets its own region
(Section~\ref{sec:dca}), so that a single extreme threshold cannot decide the
label of a whole surface. The intercept-only baseline is fitted but excluded
from every admissible set, because an increment measured against a model with
no features is inflated by construction.

\emph{Specification regret} --- what choosing one specification
costs against the best one --- is computed from the same surface but reported
only as a robustness check, because on this corpus it returns a null
(Supplement~\ref{sec:regretdef}; its reading in net benefit is in
Section~\ref{sec:nbregret}).
