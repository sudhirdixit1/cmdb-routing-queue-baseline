I read the full manuscript (97 pages, 12 sections, 10 appendices, ~35 tables), re-derived every corpus-level statistic I could from the printed tables, cross-checked numbers that appear in more than one place, and checked the submission against Elsevier's standard requirements for Information Systems (I could not fetch the Guide for Authors page directly because ScienceDirect blocks automated access, so the compliance items below are from Elsevier's standard template and the journal's stated policies; verify the keyword limit and highlights requirement on the live page before submission).

1. What the paper claims

The incremental predictive performance of a recorded field (here, a CMDB's affected CI) is not a number but a surface over a declared design space (learner, encoding, target, split, decision time, register quality, baseline, metric, operating point). The paper supplies: an exact functional-ANOVA decomposition of the surface's variance; whole-surface simultaneous max-t bands from a nested moving-block bootstrap with a multiplier-bootstrap critical value; resolution regions with a robustness index ρ; specification regret evaluated out of sample; Fieller sets for the relative reduction R; three propositions; a 19-pair benchmark over 13 public logs; a BPIC 2014 CMDB case study at two decision times; a simulation; a reporting standard; a package.

2. Fit and compliance

Scope. In scope. Information Systems publishes process mining and predictive process monitoring (Senderovich et al. 2018 and Suriadi et al. 2017 are from this journal and are cited), data quality, and evaluation methodology. The journal also has a Reproducibility Editor and a reproducibility track, which the paper's archive/harness design suits well. A reviewer could still argue the statistical core belongs in a stats or ML venue; the PPM/CMDB framing is what keeps it here, so that framing must not be diluted.

Front matter. CRediT statement, competing interests, funding, data availability, code availability, and a generative-AI declaration are all present. Keywords: seven listed; the standard Elsevier template caps keywords at six. Highlights are referenced by the linter (Appendix J) but not visible in the PDF; Elsevier requires 3–5 highlights of ≤85 characters each. Abstract is roughly 230 words, acceptable, but see style comments.

Generative AI declaration. The disclosure is unusually complete, which is good. Two items will draw editorial attention: (a) "Portions of the manuscript were drafted and edited with the assistant" goes beyond Elsevier's stated permitted use in writing (readability and language); (b) using an LLM as the sole adjudicator for the literature pilot is a use in the research method, which Elsevier permits only if described reproducibly in Methods with model name and version. The declaration names "Anthropic Claude" but no version or date. Fix both.

Length. This is the first thing an editor will react to. Ninety-seven pages, with roughly 30 of them (Appendices D, G, H, J, and much of Sections 4.2, 4.4, 10) about the project's own history of errors. Nothing in the guide forbids it, but no reviewer at this journal will read all of it, and a paper that a reviewer cannot finish is a paper a reviewer rejects on impression.

3. Strengths
The central thesis is correct and important for PPM, where benchmark protocols fix a learner, encoding and metric per experiment and report one number. The paper turns "the result depends on choices" into a measurable object with inference.
The whole-surface family for max-t bands (vs. within-cell) is the right correction and its cost is reported (critical value 3.33 vs 2.35; 9 of 19 region labels change).
The multiplier-bootstrap critical value decoupled from the refit budget is a practical and well-justified device.
The basic (pivotal) interval diagnosis in Section 4.2 — bootstrap resamples see ~63% of register levels, so the bootstrap distribution shifts below the estimate — is a real finding about high-cardinality categorical bootstraps, and the (n, K) plane in 10.4 is an honest self-limitation.
Decision time as an argument of the estimand, and the λ leakage-tipping curve (Figure 5), are the most useful things in the paper for a practitioner.
Dependent quality mechanisms (time, content, propensity) are closer to how CMDBs actually degrade than coin flips.
Reproducibility discipline is exceptional: registered protocol, amendment log, generated macros, verifier, corruption suite, provenance hashes, independent package implementation.
4. Major concerns

These are ordered by how much they threaten acceptance.

M1. The case study's headline number is internally inconsistent across the manuscript. This is the most serious problem, and it is exactly the class of error the paper's Appendix J claims to make structurally impossible.

Section 7.1, Table 13, Table 15, Figure 5, and the Conclusion state the register is worth −0.0032 [−0.0079, +0.0013] at τ₂ against intake + group + knowledge, "indistinguishable from zero."
Table 2 (PC3, "the register adds at the later decision time, after the knowledge reference exists", cell B_intake_g_km/clean/1.0) reports +0.00768 with Holm-adjusted p = 0.0025 and reject = True, i.e., the register resolvably does add at τ₂.
Table 18 (auc, B_intake_g_km) reports V = 0.008, consistent with Table 2 and not with Table 13.

Similarly, Section 7.1/Table 13 give +0.1033 against intake + group and +0.1835 against intake alone, while Table 16 ("clean 0.167"), Table 18 (V|B_intake_g = 0.167), PC2 (0.16727), Section 7.4 ("+0.1673 complete"), and PC1 (0.25718 against intake) give different values for what are described as the same cells on "the primary log at the reference cell." The λ curve's endpoint (0.104 at λ = 1) matches the Table 13 cohort; the quality tables match the other.

The likely explanation is the two-cohort issue the paper itself flags (45,455-case reassignment cohort vs 46,606-case registered cohort), but Section 7 says it uses the first, and then Tables 16–18 inside Section 7 silently use the second. Worse, PC3 being rejected means that on one cohort the "turns on the decision time" story is false: the register still resolvably adds after the knowledge reference. The abstract and conclusion quote only the cohort where the story holds. A reviewer who spots this will treat it as selective reporting, whatever the cause.

M2. Decision time is not operationally distinct from the baseline axis. Table 13 rows for τ₁/intake and τ₂/intake are identical to every digit. Nothing about the data, cohort, features, or label timing changes between τ₁ and τ₂; only the admissible baseline does. The paper argues in Section 3.1 that τ "is not the same as the split rule and it is not a data-cleaning detail," but as implemented it is a relabelling of the baseline ladder. Either implement τ so that it changes something other than B (e.g., the case's feature snapshot at that timestamp, the cohort of cases that exist at that time), or state plainly that τ enters the estimand only through the admissible set and drop the claim that it is a separate axis. Table 1 lists it as an axis; the decomposition has no τ term.

M3. The paper's own limitations remove the evidentiary force of two of its four new objects. Section 10.4 and 11 state that the reported bands undercover on the median pair (K/n = 0.106, coverage falls below 90% beyond 0.05 and below 75% by 0.125), that the region labels and ρ are therefore upper bounds on resolution, and that the "uniformly beneficial" count is computed on the 13% inference surface where it is easiest to reach. Resolution regions and ρ are two of the four new reporting objects. As it stands, a reviewer can say: the two novel objects are not reliable on the corpus they were introduced on. You need either (a) draw counts and an inference surface large enough that coverage is near nominal on at least the ITSM family, or (b) a coverage-corrected band (e.g., calibrated via the (n, K) plane) so that the region labels carry their nominal meaning, or (c) demote regions/ρ to "illustrative" and lead with the objects that don't need bands.

M4. The "misreport rate" counts sign disagreements among cells whose sign is not resolved. The 35.3% headline counts cells whose point estimate has the opposite sign from the reference cell. But Table 9 shows the median pair has 159–180 of 180 cells unresolved — their sign is noise. A conventional report is not "misstating the sign" of a cell whose sign the data cannot determine. Report M(s) restricted to resolved cells, or magnitude-weighted, or both. The unit-free framing does not rescue it: sign agreement between two draws from a distribution centred near zero is ~50% by construction, which is roughly what the worst pairs show (0.50, 0.50, 0.58).

M5. The practical stakes of "what one number costs" are thin. Out-of-sample excess regret: one-number 0.00451 AUC vs weighted-mean 0.00002. Under rolling-fold (the harder, more realistic test) the ordering reverses (0.00885 vs 0.02167). Leave-one-log-out preserves pairwise orderings 58% of the time, which is near chance. On the common operational utility (net benefit), three of four rules tie at 0.00000. The paper reports all of this honestly, but the reader is left with: the surface apparatus beats the one-number report by half a hundredth of AUC in the setting most favourable to it, loses under temporal validation, and ties in the units a desk uses. That is not enough to carry Section 6.6 as a "result worth carrying away." Either find a design where the rules separate in a decision-relevant unit (a realistic threshold distribution for a desk; a cost-weighted net benefit) or reframe the contribution as the sign-disagreement and decomposition results, with regret as a secondary check.

M6. Novelty is narrower than the length implies. The paper says so itself: fANOVA, max-t, multiplier bootstrap, DCA, Fieller, multiverse are all existing; the whole-surface family and declared-measure ANOVA are adaptations; ρ and regret are new. ρ is a difference of counts divided by a total; regret is an expected hinge loss under a decision rule. Both are simple. Three propositions: Prop. 1 is a two-point-score construction; Prop. 2 is a three-line proof under a strong richness assumption; Prop. 3 is a 3×3 example. None is wrong, but they are presented with the weight of theorems. There is no head-to-head with specification-curve analysis as practised (permutation test on the median) on the same data to show what a reader gains. Add that comparison; it will make the contribution concrete.

M7. The learner axis is confounded with the encoding axis and is nearly degenerate on 18 of 19 pairs. Table 1 lists encoding as an axis; Table 5 has no encoding column; the "four learners" are two model families × two encodings/calibrations, and only two levels run outside the case study. A sensitivity index for "learner" is then a mixture of family and encoding, and its median first-order share (3.6%) is a statement about a two-level axis. Either separate the axes or rename the axis "pipeline" and say what it contains.

M8. Corpus-level statistics I cannot reproduce from the printed tables.

"Largest first-order index at the median pair is 28.7%": the median of the per-row maxima in Table 7 is 0.263, not 0.287. If the headline is over pair×instrument combinations rather than Table 7's rows, say so and print the table it comes from.
Section 6.5: "Under the equal-level measure the median is 32.8%"; the abstract, conclusion and Table 6 say 35.3%. Which measure is Table 6's misreport column under? If Table 6 is not equal-level, its caption must say which.
Table 2, PC3: a two-sided 95% basic interval [−0.00059, 0.01210] that includes zero is not consistent with a one-sided raw bootstrap p of 0.001 (that interval implies p ≈ 0.03).
Table 5 column headers read "declared 1080 / computed cells 288." The text asserts "declared equals computed." The 288 is model-fit cells including the intercept-only rung before the ×5 instrument expansion; the 1080 is scalar cells excluding it. A reviewer who reads the table as printed concludes the audit failed. Put the two counts on the same basis or label them.
The master surface's 262,656 rows only reconcile if the intercept-only rung is included (7,296 × 36); the text says it is excluded from every admissible set. State that the master file includes it and the admissible sets do not.

M9. Excluded pairs appear in appendices. Table 3 excludes Helpdesk/handover and BPIC19/handover; Table E.24 reports both (with a base AUC of 0.400 at prevalence 0.0011 for Helpdesk/handover, which is degenerate). Remove or justify.

M10. Construct validity of some registers. Table 4 admits case:RequestedAmount (BPIC17) as a "register" — a numeric loan amount treated as a high-cardinality categorical is not a maintained entity under any reading of Section 5.2, and the paper's own "why it behaves as a register" column concedes "reuse is thin." case:parts (BPIC15) and article (RoadFines) are also stretches. The paper names these as threats, which is honest, but the headline corpus statistics (35.3%, 56.0%) include them. Report every corpus statistic twice: all 19 pairs, and the 8 ITSM pairs where the construct is what the paper is about. Section 5.4 promises this ("reported separately in Section 6") and Section 6 does not deliver it.

M11. The literature pilot should not be in the paper. Sensitivity 0.474, one machine-assisted adjudicator, κ = 0.20, convenience frame of 600 records, effective n = 29.6, and the paper's own statement that no claim rests on it. Six pages of appendix plus a paragraph in Section 9.3 for a result the paper disowns is a target for reviewers. Move it entirely to supplementary material or cut it.

M12. Presentation. The manuscript is written as a running commentary on its own previous versions. "An earlier version of this work" appears dozens of times; "correction Cnn in Appendix D" appears in the main text of Sections 3–8; Appendix D (7 pages) is a taxonomy of the author's own mistakes; Appendix J describes lint checks that caught a doubled word "Appendix." A referee for this journal already asked for this to go to the supplement, and it is still load-bearing in the main text. The prose is also heavily aphoristic ("Collapse for a decision; report the surface", "which is this paper's argument in a line", the all-caps "WHAT THIS FIGURE DOES NOT SHOW"), with frequent defensive asides addressed to an imagined referee. This will read to a second-round reviewer as combative rather than transparent. A scientific paper reports the method and the result; the changelog belongs in the repository.

5. Minor issues
Abstract: the final clause ("though on a common operational utility three of the four decision rules tie, which we report") is a hedge inside the abstract; either state the result plainly or omit.
Table 12 rows are not in the same order as every other table.
Table 4 and Table 17 have rows running together in the PDF; Table 13 and Table 2 overflow the margin.
Table 12: BPIC15_4/duration calibration slope 0.029, BPIC19/duration intercept −3.16 with slope 0.28 — these models are essentially uncalibrated, and any net-benefit or proper-scoring-rule increment on them is not interpretable even after recalibration (Table 19 shows Platt slope 0.028 for BPIC15_4). Consider excluding such pairs from the decision-curve analysis by a registered rule.
Figure 1 and Figure 6 lack labelled subpanels; Figure 6's dip at θ = 0.55 needs the sentence from Section 8.3 in the caption.
The "PC" vs "C" numbering explanation belongs in a footnote, not the body.
"n train" in Table 12 for BPIC14 (32,624) equals the case-study training count but the target/prevalence are the registered cohort's — another place where the two cohorts blur.
The repository name (cmdb-routing-queuebaseline) and reserved DOI: reviewers will attempt to clone and run REPRODUCE.md. Make sure the archived release matches the submitted numbers to the digit, given M1.
Proposition 2's Assumption 1 (every score quadruple realisable) is strong; say in one sentence what happens under a restricted model class rather than leaving it to the assumption statement.
Remove "nothing here is new" style sentences from Appendix C and elsewhere; they invite the reader to agree.
6. Verdict

Major revision. Not accept, not reject.

The reasoning: the methodological core is sound, the problem matters to this journal's readership, and the reproducibility apparatus exceeds the journal's norms. But the manuscript cannot be accepted with M1 unresolved (the load-bearing case-study number appears with two different values and two different signs, one Holm-rejected), with two of its four new objects declared unreliable on their own corpus (M3), and at a length and in a voice that no reviewer will finish. A strict reviewer would recommend reject-and-resubmit on M1 and M12 alone; a sympathetic one, major revision. I would write major revision with a note to the editor that the revision must be substantially shorter.

7. The plan to a strong accept
Phase 1 — Correctness (do this before touching anything else)
Resolve M1 completely. Decide which cohort the case study is. Run the entire Section 7 on one cohort. If you keep both, every table and every quoted number in Section 7 must carry the cohort in its caption and the text must reconcile PC3 (+0.0077, rejected) with Section 7.1 (−0.0032): "On the registered cohort the register still resolvably adds at τ₂ (+0.008); on the reassignment cohort it does not (−0.003). The decision-time result is therefore cohort-dependent, and Figure 5 is reported for the reassignment cohort." That sentence turns a contradiction into a finding. Then re-verify: this is exactly the Class F error the harness exists to prevent, so add a verifier condition that any macro named "the increment at τ₂" resolves to one value.
Fix PC3's p-value vs interval, or explain the one-sided/two-sided discrepancy.
Reconcile 28.7% / 32.8% / 35.3% and label the aggregation for each.
Rebuild Table 5 on a single basis (scalar admissible cells) with a separate column for model fits, and add a line stating the master file includes the intercept-only rung.
Remove excluded pairs from Table E.24 or re-admit them by a registered amendment.
Report all corpus statistics for all 19 and for the 8 ITSM pairs. Add a Table 6b.
Phase 2 — Strengthen the two weak objects
Coverage. Raise draws on the ITSM pairs (they are the ones you claim to speak to) until K/n-adjusted coverage is ≥ 90%, or introduce a coverage-calibrated critical value from the (n, K) plane and report regions under it. If neither is feasible, retitle Section 6.4 "Resolution regions (illustrative; see coverage caveat)" and move ρ out of the abstract.
Redefine the misreport rate on resolved cells (or magnitude-weight it) and report both. Expect the headline to fall; a smaller, defensible number beats a larger one a reviewer can dismiss.
Regret. Either find a decision-relevant design where the rules separate (e.g., a desk-elicited threshold distribution; a cost-asymmetric utility) or reframe: "the surface's value is in the decomposition and the sign-disagreement; the regret analysis shows the decision-optimal collapse is the weighted mean, and that in-sample and out-of-sample results differ." Stop calling it "the result we would most want a reader to take away."
Decision time. Implement τ as a genuine axis (feature snapshot at timestamp, or cohort-at-τ) or fold it into B and say so. If you fold it, the λ curve remains; it is your best figure.
Add a direct comparison to specification-curve analysis on two or three pairs: what does the SCA median permutation test say, and what does your region label say, and where do they disagree. This is the single most persuasive addition you can make for novelty.
Separate learner and encoding into two axes (or rename "pipeline") and rerun the decomposition; at minimum on the case study log where four levels exist.
Phase 3 — Restructure and cut (target: 35–40 pages main text)
Main text: Introduction (3 pp) → Related work (3) → Estimand and propositions (4, proofs to appendix) → Estimation and inference (6) → Benchmark design (3) → Results (8) → Case study (6) → Decision-analytic reading (3) → Simulation summary (2) → Limitations (2) → Conclusion (1).
Move to supplementary material: Appendix D (corrections), G (literature pilot, or delete), H (noisy-world diagnosis), J (harness), the full Table 21/22/23 set, Tables 17–18, Appendix E, Appendix F.
Delete every "an earlier version of this work" and "correction Cnn" from the main text. Replace with a single paragraph in Section 1 or the Conclusion: "This manuscript is the product of twenty analysis rounds; a correction register and referee log are in the archive." The archive still gets the credit; the paper stops reading like a confession.
Rewrite in plain scientific register. Cut the aphorisms, the caps, the "which is the whole point of the following", the "we say so rather than letting a reader assume." Each of these was written to pre-empt a referee; collectively they signal a paper that expects to be attacked.
Abstract: state the estimand, the four objects, three numbers (baseline spread, higher-order share, misreport rate on resolved cells), and the case-study result with its cohort dependence. Under 200 words.
Front matter: six keywords; 3–5 highlights ≤85 characters; add model name/version/date to the AI declaration; tighten "drafted" to language and readability editing if that is accurate, or describe the drafting use in a way that meets Elsevier's policy.
Phase 4 — Make the case study land for this journal's readers

Since the CMDB is the motivating case and the journal's audience includes ITSM and process-mining practitioners, Section 7 should translate the register layers into terms the field uses. Appendix E's coarsenings (CI → CI type → subtype → service component) map directly onto CSDM-style layering (CI class hierarchy → application service / service offering), and the finding that "coarse layers are close to free, the item is what pays" is a concrete statement about where CMDB population effort has predictive payoff. One paragraph relating the register-quality mechanisms to how discovery, reconciliation and staleness actually arise in a CMDB (discovery lag for new services, unreconciled duplicate CIs from multiple sources, affected-CI populated by the desk rather than by discovery) would make Table 17 legible to that reader and would strengthen the paper's claim to be in scope. Keep it short and keep the disclaimer that this is predictive performance, not procurement advice.
