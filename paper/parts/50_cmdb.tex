\section{Case study: forecasting incident reassignment at a bank's service desk}
\label{sec:cmdb}

This section evaluates one register against a named forecasting decision on
a public log: will this incident be reassigned to another group before it is
resolved? --- a binary forecast issued at a declared origin. The log is BPI
Challenge 2014 \citep{vandongen2014bpic}, from a bank's IT service desk; the
register is its configuration management database, the inventory of the IT
items incidents concern, and the register field is the affected
configuration item, which takes \cardTauTwo\ distinct values on the
case-study cohort at incident creation. We report it in detail because it is
the only log on which the forecast origin is varied, and there the origin
decides the answer.

\subsection{One log, two answers, and what separates them}
\label{sec:cohort}

This log appears twice. Section~\ref{sec:multilog} applies the pre-specified
generic rules of Section~\ref{sec:design}, which admit \nRegisteredCohort\
cases and define the target by the pre-specified handover rule. This section
uses the cohort and the reassignment target defined for the service desk:
\nCohort\ cases at prevalence \casePrevalence\ (\casePrevalenceTest\ in the
test half), \nCaseTrain\ in training and \nCaseTest\ in test. The case-study
cohort is a strict subset of the pre-specified one: the \nExtraCases\ cases
the pre-specified rules admit on top of it are those the desk's own cutoff
excludes.

The two give different answers about the register at the later origin,
though not answers anybody would act on differently. Against intake plus group
plus the knowledge reference, the case-study cohort and reassignment target
give \VTtwoKnow\ and the pre-specified cohort and handover target give
\VregisteredTauTwo: opposite signs, both inside the \mpidAuc\ AUC that
Section~\ref{sec:results-regret} declares the minimal practically important
difference. On the second, the pre-specified contrast that the register
adds at the later origin (\textsf{PC3}, Supplement~\ref{sec:planned}) has a one-sided
$p$-value that rejects at the Holm-adjusted level while its interval does not
exclude zero; the interval decides, and the supplement says why the two
disagree. Table~\ref{tab:cohort} reconciles, one factor at a time, the
value an earlier, unpublished analysis of this log reported with the
pre-specified one.

\textbf{It is the target, not the cohort.} Over the cohort $\times$ target
design the target carries \shareTargetPct\ of the variance in the increment,
the cohort \shareCohortPct, and their interaction \shareCohortTargetPct.
Reassignment to another group before resolution has prevalence
\casePrevalence\ over the case-study cohort; handover between activity
groups, under the pre-specified rule, has prevalence \prevRegisteredHandover\
over the pre-specified cohort. They are not the same question and the
register is not worth the same to them. It is not a data-preparation
difference --- the two analyses reach the same attribute values on every
row --- nor a modelling difference, since they run the same pipeline at the
same reference cell.

\textbf{The tie-break is declared.} \nTiedTimestamps\ incidents share their
timestamp with another, so the temporal split at seventy per cent of the row
order depends on a tie-breaking rule; over \nTiebreakDraws\ random
tie-breaks the increment ranges over \tiebreakRange\ AUC without changing
sign, and every number in this section uses a declared tie-break by incident
identifier (Supplement~\ref{app:taudetail}).

\subsection{Three forecast origins}
\label{sec:twodecision}

A forecast origin fixes three things at once --- which cases are in scope,
what is known about them, and which fields are admissible --- and the
case-study surface is estimated at three origins.

\textbf{The register's worth turns on which origin the forecast is issued
at}: at $\tau_0$ it is \VtauZero\ [\VtauZeroLo, \VtauZeroHi] over
\nTauZero\ \emph{interactions} --- calls to the desk, of which only a
minority ever become incidents, which is why that population is far larger
than the \nTauTwo\ incidents the later origins carry --- at $\tau_1$
\VtauOne\ [\VtauOneLo, \VtauOneHi], and at $\tau_2$ against the intake block
plus the group it is \VTtwoGroup\ [\VTtwoGroupLo, \VTtwoGroupHi] while
against the block, the group and the knowledge reference it is \VTtwoKnow\
[\VTtwoKnowLo, \VTtwoKnowHi] --- not resolvably different from zero. The step
from $\tau_1$ to $\tau_2$ is an information step only on the matched
population: \nTauTwoUnmatched\ of the \nTauTwo\ incidents have no interaction
record, so the \tauMatchedRow\ row of Table~\ref{tab:tau} restricts $\tau_2$
to $\tau_1$'s own cases. Read as steps, the increment moves by
\deltaCohortAtTau\ AUC from $\tau_0$ to $\tau_1$, which is the population; by
\deltaSnapshotAtTau\ from $\tau_1$ to $\tau_2$ on the matched population,
which is the information; and by \deltaPopulationResidual\ more from the
matched population to the full one, which is the incidents without an
interaction record (Supplement~\ref{app:taudetail}).

\label{sec:quality}
Two further experiments are in Supplement~\ref{app:casedetail}. The first is
an information-set (look-ahead) sensitivity: writing $\lambda$ for the share
of the knowledge reference's values assumed written \emph{after} the
incident, the increment reaches half its knowledge-free value at
$\lambda = \lambdaHalf$, so an unprovable admissibility assumption is
reported as a sensitivity analysis and not as a headline. The second is a
register-quality axis under the \nQualityKinds\ declared mechanisms and,
beside them, three dependent mechanisms and a discovery lag, applied to both
halves of the data with their parameters estimated from training alone
(Supplement~\ref{app:degrade}).
