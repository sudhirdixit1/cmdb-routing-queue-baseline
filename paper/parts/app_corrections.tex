
This section is a corrections register: the summaries whose computation was
changed during the analysis, each with the old and the new value where there
is one, and the reason for the change. Entries are grouped by \emph{class},
because the classes say what kind of mistake an apparatus of this kind is
prone to. Identifiers run in the order the changes were made; three of the
low numbers were never assigned, so the classes below are not a contiguous
range, which is a fact about the numbering and not a missing entry.

\paragraph{Class A --- a control that could not fail}
Four corrections (C1, C2, C8, C16) are nulls or searches drawn at the wrong
level, swept over the wrong range, or given fresh noise inside a comparison
that assumed determinism. A null that cannot fail is worse than no null,
because it is reported as evidence. In C16 the axis non-redundancy search
drew fresh noise inside each cell evaluation, so two evaluations of the same
cell disagreed and the search found almost nothing; the search was later
removed altogether (C24).

\paragraph{Class B --- a quantity whose sign was set by a tie-break}
Two corrections (C4, C7) concern an operational factor computed at a fixed
review capacity, where $93.1\%$ of what the naive baseline nominated came from
a single tied block and reordering rows inside that block moved the arm from
$-26$ to $+608$. A quantity whose sign is set by a tie-break is not a
measurement. Section~\ref{sec:dca} is built on net benefit, which admits or
excludes a whole tied block at a threshold and never breaks one.

\paragraph{Class C --- every literal correct, the relation between them
false}
Five corrections (C9, C10, C12, C13, C14) are sentences in which each printed
number was right and the claim asserted about them was not: an extremum that
was the worst of the points the table happened to name; a count reported as a
contiguous run; a range ``across the operating range'' that was a range across
five named points; a universal ``if and only if'' whose necessity direction is
only existential (Section~\ref{app:props}); and --- the one that mattered most
--- a headline spread inflated by an intercept-only baseline no analyst would
build, which a later section of the same manuscript acknowledged and the
abstract did not. This class is the reason
Section~\ref{sec:regionsdef} excludes the intercept-only baseline by definition and
the reason every number in this document is a generated macro rather than a
typed literal.

\paragraph{Class D --- evidence one download away}
One correction (C11). The analysis had reported that it could not establish
whether the knowledge-article reference was available at incident creation,
and had named the file that would settle where the field comes from without
obtaining it. Obtaining it changed the headline. Section~\ref{sec:cmdb}
reports the register at three forecast origins (Table~\ref{tab:tau}) rather
than choosing one, because the file settled where the field is written and
not when.

\paragraph{Class E --- a claim about the world that our own coder could not
support}
One correction (C15), and it is the largest. The old statement was that
\emph{not one} of twenty audited papers reports the increment across a range
of operating points. The rebuilt pilot of Section~\ref{sec:practice}
adjudicates the papers the mechanical screen \emph{rejected} as well as the
ones it accepted, measures the screen's sensitivity at \auditSens, and finds
eligible papers that do report the increment across a range of operating
points --- one of them at four values of $k$ in a top-$k$ evaluation. The old
``not one'' was a property of a coder that finds fewer than half of the
eligible papers, not a property of the literature. The new estimate is
\auditRangeThresholdPct\ with an interval that includes it.

\paragraph{Class F --- internal inconsistency}
Four corrections (C17, C18, C19, C22) are places where the manuscript
disagreed with itself: a metric count that treated two thresholds of one
metric as two metrics; a reference reduction quoted as
$43.7\%$ in the text and $35.0\%$ in a table without the change of
specification being made clear; and a section that said a metric
removed from the analysis was retained in a table that did not contain it.
The generated-macro discipline of Section~\ref{app:verifier} makes this class
structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely --- except when the same
wrong quantity is computed twice from the same wrong expression, which is
C22: the pilot's frame size was corrected in the facts file and not in the
PRISMA flow, so Section~\ref{sec:practice} read \nAuditFrame\ and
Table~\ref{tab:prisma} two orders of magnitude more. The frame is now
computed once, before both consumers, and the verifier checks every row of
the flow that the prose also quotes.

\paragraph{Class G --- an estimand that was not the one being estimated}
One correction (C20), the subject of Section~\ref{app:noisy}. The simulation
of Section~\ref{sec:sim} computed both estimands by enumerating the data
generator's cells, which in the noisy world carry the register's \emph{true}
value while the estimator only ever observes a value replaced by a uniform
draw with probability \simNoiseRate. Coverage was therefore measured against
a population the estimator does not sample from: \coverageNoisyBefore, with
an apparent bias of \biasNoisyBefore. The change is exact marginalisation of
the noise, under which coverage is \coverageNoisyAfter; the estimator's mean
is \meanNoisyEstimate\ under either target.

Nothing in the generated-macro discipline can catch this class: every number
was internally consistent, correctly computed and correctly printed, and what
was wrong was the \emph{definition} of the quantity two of them referred to.
It was found by two diagnostics, each able to confirm the reading that the
method fails in that world, which both refused to confirm
(Section~\ref{app:noisy}). That is not automatable.

\paragraph{Class H --- a statistic aggregated over the wrong index set}
One correction (C21). The literature pilot's frame size summed a stratum-size
column over the \emph{rows} of the sample rather than over its distinct
strata, and a stratum's size is carried on every row of that stratum. The
quantity computed was $\sum_h N_h n_h$ and the quantity intended was
$\sum_h N_h$, so a frame of \nAuditFrame\ records was reported as one two
orders of magnitude larger. Every input was correct; the arithmetic was
correct; the group-by was wrong.

The correction changed what the pilot \emph{is} and not only how large it
sounds. With the true frame in view the design weight is \auditMaxWeight\ in
every stratum, the enumeration stopped short of its declared budget of
\nAuditTarget, and the pilot is therefore a census of what one index returned
rather than a probability sample of a literature, as Section~\ref{app:pilot}
states, with the additional limit that the frame's coverage of the field is
unmeasured. The guard is a re-derivation rather than a recount: the verifier
computes the frame from the sample by grouping, and refuses a manuscript in
which a frame is smaller than the sample it contains, or differs from it while
every design weight is one.

\paragraph{Class I --- an inferential object whose denominator was not the
one the claim quantified over}
Two corrections (C26, C28), the same mistake in two places. A region label
such as \emph{uniformly beneficial} ranges over every admissible cell of a
surface, and it had been supported by max-$t$ bands whose family was the five
scalar metrics \emph{at one cell}, two orders of magnitude smaller than
the claim. Widening the family to the whole surface raises the critical value
from \maxTWithinMedian\ to \maxTSurfaceMedian\ at the median pair and changes
the region label on \nRegionChangedByFamily\ of \nPairs\ pairs --- and more at
the cell level, where the wider family unresolves cells throughout.
Section~\ref{sec:simbands} declares four interval objects by name so that a
table cannot print one and be read as the other. The same class covers the
pilot's Wilson intervals (C28): a binomial interval was put around a ratio of
two weighted totals through a fractional ``effective sample size'', and none
of the five resulting intervals is contained in its design-based counterpart.
An interval is only as good as the correspondence between the family it
controls and the sentence it is used to license, and that correspondence is a
judgement about language, not a property of the arithmetic.

\paragraph{Class J --- a quantity assembled from units that do not add}
Two corrections (C25, C27). The first: the largest per-axis difference
$\max_i(S_{T_i} - S_i)$ had been described as the share of variance carried by
interactions. Those differences overlap across axes --- a two-way component
appears in the total effect of both of its axes --- so their maximum is
neither their sum nor their union, and the total higher-order share is
$1 - \sum_i S_i$, which on this corpus is \interactionTotalPct\ against the
\largestInvolvementPct\ that was printed. The second: expected regret was
averaged over an admissible set spanning five scalar metrics and reported
as a single percentage ``in the metric's own units''. There is no such unit
once AUC and Brier skill are inside one average. Section~\ref{sec:rules}
reports regret inside one metric at a time, and
Section~\ref{sec:nbregret} reports a common-utility version on calibrated net
benefit, a scale on which averaging is defined.

\paragraph{Class K --- a theorem stated wider than its proof, and wider than
the truth}
Two corrections (C23, C24). The necessity direction of the rank-invariance
proposition quantified over every non-rank-based metric and was supported
by one stored configuration for five named metrics under one map. Repairing
the proof also refuted the claim: what invariance of the \emph{reduction}
requires is that the recalibration act \emph{affinely} on the metric, not
that it leave it fixed, and Section~\ref{app:props} exhibits a
non-rank-based metric whose reduction is invariant. The word
\emph{exactly} was wrong, not merely unproved. C24 removed a labelled
computational result: a search over a family of synthetic service desks for
witnesses that no design axis determines another. Its answer moved between
zero and five of twelve ordered pairs as an arbitrary equality tolerance was
swept, failing to find a witness is not evidence that one axis determines
another, and finding witnesses in a finite synthetic family is not a
non-redundancy theorem. No claim depended on it.

\paragraph{Class L --- a number the design cannot produce, and a word the
design does not license}
Two corrections (C29, C30). Raw and Holm-adjusted bootstrap $p$-values of
$0.000$ from 200 draws had been printed; no finite bootstrap can produce zero,
and Section~\ref{sec:planned} uses the plus-one estimator with the smallest
attainable value stated beside it. And the five contrasts had been called
\emph{confirmatory}, although they were fixed after earlier analyses of the
same data; they are pre-specified exploratory contrasts, which is what the
design supports.

\paragraph{Class M --- an interpretation attached to a scale that does not
carry it}
One correction (C31). Decision-curve thresholds were read as probability
thresholds, and therefore as cost ratios, on scores whose calibration slope
was reported in the same manuscript as far from one.
Section~\ref{sec:calibfirst} refits every model contributing to a
decision-curve conclusion under a training-only calibration and labels
raw-score curves as score-threshold sensitivity analyses, which is what they
are.

\paragraph{Class N --- a summary computed on a set no table prints}
Three corrections (C32, C33, C34), all made after release v28.0 was
cut, and all of the kind Class H names with a narrower cause: the sentence
quoted a number as being in a table while the macro behind it was computed on
rows that table does not print. The layer values for BPIC19
(Section~\ref{app:layers}) were taken from the handover pair, which the
pre-specified rules exclude and Table~\ref{tab:layers} does not print, and their
``coarse'' maximum left out the vendor, the one layer on that log worth
anything; the macros now read the admitted pair and name the vendor. The
minimum unseen-category rate (Section~\ref{app:calibfull}) was read without
the baseline filter, from the intercept-only baseline no table prints, and printed as
a rate no pair has; it now carries Table~\ref{tab:calibration}'s own filter.
And the case-study quality tables said their stochastic mechanisms were
averaged over the corpus run's seed count when the case-study run had its
own, smaller one; the caption now reads that run's count. None moves a number
the article quotes; release v28.0 would print the old values, and the release
the code-availability statement names prints the corrected ones.

\paragraph{How these were found}
The estimand error (C20) was found by two diagnostics built so that either
could have confirmed the comfortable reading; the aggregation error (C21) and
the inconsistency it left behind (C22) by reading the compiled pages, which
is also how the typesetting defects that the linter of
Section~\ref{app:verifier} now checks for were found. An apparatus that
guarantees every number is derived from a result file guarantees nothing
about whether the sentence around it says what was meant; that is checked by
reading the output.
