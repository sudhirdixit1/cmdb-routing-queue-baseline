
\section{The pre-specified benchmark design}
\label{sec:design}

Which datasets enter, which recorded attribute plays the predictor, which the
forecast target and which the baseline were fixed by written rules before any
result on a new log existed.

\subsection{What was fixed, and when}

The admission rules and targets were fixed in a dated protocol before any
result on a new log existed, and six later amendments are logged with their causes
and the results they changed (Supplement~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{This is
not a timestamped third-party registration}: the protocol is a file in a
repository whose history is under the analyst's own control, so a claim that
a rule was fixed in advance is no stronger than that repository. What the
design buys is that the rules are legible, dated and amendable only on the
record.

\subsection{Roles}

A log is admitted if the pre-specified rules can find three things in it
without reading an outcome.

\begin{description}
\item[The register $f$.] A high-cardinality attribute that is not
resource-like, has at least \minCardF\ distinct values, is reused across at
least \minReuseF\ cases on average, and --- by amendment~1 --- whose values
recur across the temporal split, so that a document number is not mistaken for
a maintained entity.
\item[The free field $g$.] A resource-like attribute (on the case-study log,
the assignment group), present on at least \minGPresent\ of cases, whose
value varies within a case. Only the \emph{first} value it takes on a case is
used as a feature, while the handover target is defined from the whole
sequence of values --- so the feature is what is known when the case opens,
the target is what happens afterwards, and the field cannot inform the model
about its own future.
\item[The intake block $B_0$.] Every remaining attribute of cardinality at
most \maxCardBZero\ that is neither an outcome, nor a timestamp, nor a
relabelling of the activity, nor a deterministic coarsening of $f$.
\end{description}

The nested baselines are built from these roles: the intercept-only model;
half of the intake block (its lower-cardinality half, fixed by a
deterministic order before any outcome is seen); the whole intake block; the
block plus the free field $g$; and, where a log admits a further field, that
field as a baseline of its own --- on the primary log the knowledge
reference, a pointer to a knowledge-base article. The intercept-only baseline
is in the surface and excluded from every admissible set
(Table~\ref{tab:axes}).

Table~\ref{tab:roles} gives, per pair, the attribute playing each role and the
number of levels each axis carries; Table~\ref{tab:master} repeats each
pair's admitted case count and register cardinality $K$. The \emph{target}
indexes surfaces rather than varying within one, and the per-pair factorial
is \nPerPairAxes\ axes wide, not the \nAxes\ of Definition~\ref{def:space}:
the forecast origin varies only on the case study's log, the operating point
only in the decision-curve family, and the cohort only in
Section~\ref{sec:cohort} (Section~\ref{sec:lim}).

\subsection{Targets, splits and exclusions}
\label{sec:targets}

Two targets are defined for every log --- \emph{handover}, whether more than
one resource group touches the case, and \emph{duration}, whether it runs
longer than the training half's median. Cases are ordered by first timestamp
and split $70/30$, with \nFolds\ expanding-window folds beside it, and fields
determined only at closure are excluded by name
\citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage,weytjens2022leakage}. A pair is
excluded only for a pre-specified reason, and every admitted pair is
analysed and reported --- running the surface only where the reduction
resolves would condition the analysis on the outcome.
Supplement~\ref{app:targets} gives the rules and the exclusion codes, and
Supplement~\ref{app:margin} the admission margin. The case-study log's
handover pair sits at the upper edge of the prevalence window, at
\prevAdmittedMax\ against a window that closes at \prevHi, and carries
\nCellsBpicFourteenHandover\ admissible cells, so every headline is also
reported on the IT service management (ITSM) family
(Supplement~\ref{sec:family}). The corpus is \nLogs\ public logs spanning
\nDomains\ domains: a \emph{benchmark of specification sensitivity across
heterogeneous forecasting problems}, not a replication of one finding, so no
effect magnitude is averaged across domains
(Supplement~\ref{app:construct}).
