
\section{Decision-analytic evaluation}
\label{sec:dca}

An increment in AUC is not a quantity a service desk can act on. This section
reads the same surface in net benefit, which is: the number of correctly
nominated cases per thousand arrivals, net of the false nominations, at a
declared exchange rate between the two.

\subsection{The operating point is a cost ratio, not a knob}

At threshold $\theta$ the net benefit of a rule that nominates when
$\hat p \geq \theta$ is
\[
\mathrm{NB}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{TP}(\theta)}{n}
\;-\; \frac{\mathrm{FP}(\theta)}{n}\cdot\frac{\theta}{1-\theta},
\]
so $(1-\theta)/\theta$ is the number of false nominations the desk is willing
to accept for one true one. Choosing $\theta$ is choosing an operational cost
ratio, and it belongs to the analyst's declaration rather than to a grid
search. We report the whole grid --- \nGrid\ points from \gridLo\ to
\gridHi\ --- and name three points a desk might occupy, each with the ratio it
encodes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
$\theta$ & false accepted per true & the desk this describes \\
\midrule
\thetaCheap & \ratioCheap & a review is cheap; nominate liberally \\
\thetaBase  & \ratioBase  & a review and a miss cost about the same \\
\thetaDear  & \ratioDear  & review capacity is scarce \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent A reader whose desk sits at a ratio not in that list reads their own
point off Figure~\ref{fig:dca}. Reporting the grid is what makes that
possible, and it is the same argument as the rest of the paper one level
down: the operating point is the reader's to choose, so the analyst reports
the curve rather than a point on it.

Figure~\ref{fig:dca} draws the register's net-benefit increment across the
grid at the reference cell, with the pointwise interval and the simultaneous
max-$t$ band. The difference is not cosmetic. The band's median width is
\dcBandWidth\ against the pointwise interval's \dcPointWidth, and across the
\nDcGrid\ operating points of this estate's decision curve the pointwise
interval declares the increment resolvably positive at
\nBeneficialPointwise\ of them while the simultaneous band declares it at
\nBeneficialSimultaneous. Those three points are the price of a statement
about a \emph{range} of thresholds rather than about each threshold
separately, and a paper that reads a decision curve across its range and
prices it pointwise has claimed the first while paying for the second.

Neither construction resolves any point of this curve as harmful ---
\nHarmfulPointwise\ pointwise and \nHarmfulSimultaneous\ simultaneously ---
which is worth saying because the curve dips visibly below zero near
$\theta = 0.55$ and a reader looking at Figure~\ref{fig:dca} would be entitled
to wonder.

\subsection{What the operational statement does not say}

Net benefit converts a discrimination increment into units the desk
recognises. It does not convert it into money, and three gaps remain, each of
which would need data this study does not have.

First, nominating a case for review is not the same as preventing a
reassignment. Net benefit counts correct nominations; whether acting on one
changes the outcome is an interventional question and this is observational
evidence.

Second, the exchange rate is declared, not measured. A desk's true ratio of
review cost to reassignment cost is an organisational quantity; we report the
whole curve so that a reader who knows theirs can read off their own point,
and we do not pretend to know it.

Third, the cost of the register itself --- discovery tooling, reconciliation
effort, the staff who keep it current --- is nowhere in this evidence. A
register that is worth \VTtwoGroup\ AUC on one task may still be worth buying
for a dozen other reasons, and may not be worth buying for this one. Nothing
here settles that, and Section~\ref{sec:lim} says so again because it is the
inference a reader is most likely to draw and least entitled to.
