\section{Simulation against a known answer}
\label{sec:sim}

Everything so far has been measured on real logs, where the answer is not
known. This section measures the estimator where it is. The full tables are in
Supplement~\ref{app:tables} and the diagnosis of the one world that failed is
in Supplement~\ref{app:noisy}.

\subsection{Six worlds, two estimands}
\label{sec:simworlds}

The simulation uses \nWorlds\ worlds --- four of them misspecified for the
estimator --- at \nSimReps\ replicates each, with the covariate space finite
so that the population AUC of any scoring rule is computed in closed form
rather than simulated. Two estimands are separated because under
misspecification they differ: $V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$, the increment between
the two Bayes-optimal rules, and $V_{\mathrm{limit}}$, the increment between
the two fitted pipelines' limits. A resampling interval can only cover the
second, and reporting its coverage of the first as the estimator's coverage
is a category error. Supplement~\ref{app:worlds} describes the worlds and the
marginalisation a corrupted register forces; Table~\ref{tab:sim31} reports
both estimands.

\subsection{Coverage, bias and width}
\label{sec:simcoverage}

Coverage estimates carry a Monte Carlo standard error of about
$\simCoverageSE$ percentage points at \nSimReps\ replicates, which is the
resolution every comparison here should be read at;
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} draws them by construction and world.

\textbf{Where the refit helps, and where the percentile interval fails.} The
nested basic interval covers a median \coverageNestedBasicMedian\ against the
fixed-model interval's \coverageNaiveMedian; on the sparse world the
\emph{percentile} interval collapses to \coverageSparsePct\ and the basic
construction restores it to \coverageSparseBasic, which is why every interval
and band here is the basic one (Supplement~\ref{app:refit};
Table~\ref{tab:sim31}).

\textbf{What no interval repairs.} Against $V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ rather than
$V_{\mathrm{limit}}$, every construction undercovers on the drifting world ---
\coverageDriftOracle\ at best --- and it should: when the coefficients move
between eras the estimator is not consistent for the oracle quantity, and the
largest gap is \biasOracleMax\ in AUC units. Under misspecification an
interval around the estimator's own limit is not an interval around the
quantity a reader cares about, and no resampling scheme repairs that.

\subsection{Sample size, register cardinality and block length}
\label{sec:simsensitivity}

A construction that works at one sample size, one register cardinality and one
block length has been validated at a point, not in a regime. Sample size and
register cardinality are therefore varied \emph{jointly}, up to $K = \simKMax$
levels --- the case study's cardinality on the registered cohort of
Section~\ref{sec:design} --- and the block length is varied around its rule
of thumb.

\textbf{The ratio $K/n$ captures most of the dependence, and not all of it.}
Across the plane the basic interval covers a median \coverageGridMedian\ and
a minimum \coverageGridMin, the last at a ratio of \simGridWorstRatio, and it
is short of nominal everywhere. It is not sufficient: two cells at nearly the
same ratio cover \simGridSameRatioCovA\ and \simGridSameRatioCovB, a gap of
\simGridSameRatioGapSE\ combined Monte Carlo standard errors, and holding the
register fixed while moving the training half from \simGridFixedNa\ rows to
\simGridFixedNb\ moves coverage from \simGridFixedCovA\ to
\simGridFixedCovB. \textbf{This corpus sits in the regime where that
matters}: its median pair has $K/n = \corpusRatioMedian$ and
\nPairsRatioAboveTenth\ of \nPairs\ pairs are above a tenth, so a nominal
band on them is \emph{narrower} than a $95\%$ interval should be.
Supplement~\ref{app:calibration} derives a calibration against the plane in
full, and Section~\ref{sec:simband} reports the measurement that keeps it
from being applied.

\textbf{Where the interval stops containing its own estimate.} On the denser
plane Supplement~\ref{app:calibration} is fitted on, at a fixed ratio of
\straddleRatio\ the basic interval straddles its own point estimate in every
replicate up to \nStraddleAllMax\ training rows and in only
\shareStraddleAtHalfPct\ of them by \nStraddleHalfMin, because the bootstrap
displacement is a bias that does not shrink with $n$ while the half-width
does; past that crossing a widening about the estimate is undefined, which
is the case on \nPlaneCellsUndefined\ of the plane's \nPlaneCellsOffered\
cells, all at \nStraddleHalfMin\ rows or more. \textbf{Block length.} Of the
\nSimBlockLengths\ lengths examined --- half the rule
$\lceil n^{1/3} \rceil$, the rule, and twice it --- coverage moves by up to
\coverageBlockSpreadMax\ within a world, and the basic interval beats the
percentile interval on the sparse register by at least \blockSparseGapMin\ at
every one.

\subsection{The band's family-wise coverage}
\label{sec:simband}

Everything above validates a \emph{pointwise} interval. Two of this paper's
three reporting objects are functions of a \emph{simultaneous} band, and this
section measures what the band's critical value covers.

\textbf{The design, and why its answer is an upper bound.} One draw from the
sampling distribution of a synthetic family gives the point estimate, and as
many further draws \emph{from that same distribution} as the corpus family it
is matched to carries give the bootstrap --- so the bootstrap is not an
approximation of the sampling distribution but is it. Every way a resampling
scheme can be wrong --- dependence it fails to reproduce, a refit that comes
apart, a block length misjudged --- is switched off, and what is left is the
object under test: a standard error and a critical value both estimated from
the same handful of draws. A coverage measured here is therefore the most the
real procedure can attain, and a shortfall here is one that no improvement
to the resampling repairs. The families are the ones the corpus runs ---
every pair's surface family of \nCellsPerPairMedian\ cells at
\nDrawsSurface\ draws, the modal decision-curve family of
\nDcaFamilyModal\ cells at the same count, and the case study's own curve
family of \nDcaFamily\ cells at \nDcaDraws\ (Table~\ref{tab:bandcov}) ---
matched in cross-cell correlation, effective rank, per-cell excess kurtosis
and --- the statistic the verdict turns on --- the ratio of the empirical
quantile to the multiplier on the corpus's own families, by grid search
against the corpus's draw files. The surface family reproduces that ratio at
\ratioSynthWhole\ against the corpus's median \ratioCorpusWhole\ (interquartile
range \ratioCorpusWholeQone\ to \ratioCorpusWholeQthree), inside the range;
the curve family reaches \ratioSynthDca\ against \ratioCorpusDca, outside it,
so its numbers below transfer less well and are read with that stated. Four regimes
are run: Gaussian draws, the case the multiplier is derived for; draws
carrying this corpus's tails, whose median cell has an excess kurtosis of
\corpusKurtMedWhole\ on the surface families and \corpusKurtNinetyWhole\ at
the ninetieth percentile; the same with the degenerate cells described
below; and a fixed heterogeneous \emph{non-zero} truth, under which a cell
can be resolved correctly and family-wise coverage is the quantity a region
label actually depends on. Coverage is over \nBandCoverageReps\ replicates
in each of \nBandCoverageCells\ grid cells, so no coverage here is
determined worse than \bandCoverageSEMax.

\textbf{The empirical quantile attains its level on the matched family; the
multiplier does not, and no draw budget makes it.} On every pair's surface
family, under this corpus's tails, the empirical quantile of the observed
maxima covers \covEmpHeavyWholeAtDesign\ against a nominal $95\%$, at a
Monte Carlo standard error of \covEmpHeavyWholeAtDesignSE, and
\covEmpNonzeroWholeAtDesign\ under the non-zero truth; the order-statistic
upper end of the same quantile covers \covEmpHiHeavyWholeAtDesign. The
Gaussian multiplier covers \covMultHeavyWholeAtDesign\ and
\covMultNonzeroWholeAtDesign\ on the same families --- four to six standard
errors short --- against \covMultGaussWholeAtDesign\ under the Gaussian draws
it is derived for, where the empirical quantile covers
\covEmpGaussWholeAtDesign: the two change places where the draws are
Gaussian, which is what the theory says and what the corpus's draws are not;
the upper end of the multiplier's Monte Carlo interval
moves that by less than a point, because it controls the error in
estimating $q$ and not the error in what $q$ estimates, and a Rademacher
wild multiplier, which keeps the observed draws' magnitudes, covers
\covRadHeavyWholeAtDesign. Along a ladder of draw counts at the surface
family the multiplier rises steeply out of the smallest budgets and then
\emph{plateaus}: \covMultBootThirtyThree\ at \nBootThirtyThree\ draws,
\covMultBootOneFifty\ at \nBootOneFifty, \covMultBootFourHundred\ at
\nBootFourHundred\ and \covMultBootOneThousand\ at \nBootOneThousand. The
empirical quantile behaves differently along the same ladder ---
\covEmpBootThirtyThree, \covEmpBootOneFifty, \covEmpBootFourHundred\ and
\covEmpBootOneThousand\ --- because an order statistic of \nBootThirtyThree\
maxima is badly determined and one of \nDrawsSurface\ is not, which is why
the construction the paper reports is available at this corpus's draw count
and would not have been at a third of it. The per-cell interval underneath
moves \covPercellBootThirtyThree\ to \covPercellBootOneThousand\ over the
same range, so part of what any band loses it loses before a critical value
is chosen. What the level costs is width: the empirical band is
\widthEmpOverMultAtDesign\ times as wide as the multiplier's, and
Section~\ref{sec:regions} prints what each resolves.

\textbf{The decision-curve families, and the cells that are zero by
arithmetic.} At a threshold where both arms treat every case alike the
net-benefit difference is zero by arithmetic, and \nDegenerateCellsDca\
cells of the corpus's curve families are exactly zero in at least nine
draws of ten, against \nDegenerateCellsWhole\ anywhere in the surface
families. Standardising such a cell by its vanishing spread sends its
studentised value to the largest value a deviate of $B$ points can take, so
it inflates the critical value every other cell is judged by while its own
band is trivially right; the degenerate regime reproduces that, and under
it the multiplier covers \covMultDegenDcaAtDesign\ on the modal curve
family. A declared rule therefore removes such cells from a decision-curve
family before the quantile is taken --- a cell that is exactly zero in at
least nine draws of ten is not a statement about the register --- and
Section~\ref{sec:dcabands} reports the case study's curve under the
empirical quantile of the admissible family, with the excluded count
printed. On the modal curve family so treated the empirical quantile covers
\covEmpHeavyDcaAtDesign\ under the corpus's tails and
\covEmpNonzeroDcaAtDesign\ under a non-zero truth, against the multiplier's
\covMultHeavyDcaAtDesign\ and \covMultNonzeroDcaAtDesign; on the case
study's own family, \covEmpHeavyCaseAtDesign\ against
\covMultHeavyCaseAtDesign. \textbf{On the curve families the empirical
quantile is therefore about a point short of its level}, on a synthetic
family that under-reproduces the corpus's estimator ratio, and the article
reports it as the better of the two estimators rather than as one measured
at nominal; Section~\ref{sec:dcabands} says so where the counts are
printed. Supplement~\ref{app:degencells} carries the
mechanism and Table~\ref{tab:bandcov} every regime and candidate.

\textbf{What the $(n, K)$ calibration is, now.} Supplement~\ref{app:calibration}
derives a widening for the \emph{pointwise} interval as a function of the
register's cardinality over the training size, from the plane of
Section~\ref{sec:simsensitivity}. It addresses the channel this section
switches off, is fitted pointwise and applied simultaneously, and is
measurably misspecified in $n$; the article reports the labels under it as
a sensitivity ladder (Supplement~\ref{app:foursettings}) and does not apply
it, and Section~\ref{sec:lim} says what that leaves open.

\paragraph{What the simulation does not establish}
It certifies the pointwise constructions on \nWorlds\ worlds, a plane of
\nSimGridCells\ $(n, K)$ cells and \nSimBlockLengths\ block lengths, and
the band's critical value on the \nBandCoverageFamilies\ matched families of
Section~\ref{sec:simband} under four regimes --- on nothing else. The stationarity and mixing the moving-block bootstrap needs are
assumed and not tested; the split point is fixed within a draw, so
split-position uncertainty is outside every interval reported here; and the
drifting world shows what happens when the estimator is not consistent for the
quantity a reader cares about.
