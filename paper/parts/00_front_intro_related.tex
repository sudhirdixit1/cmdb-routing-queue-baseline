\title{Specification Surfaces for Incremental Predictive Performance:
Estimation, Uncertainty, and Multi-Log Evaluation}

\author[ind]{Sudhir Dixit}
\ead{sudhir.dixit1@gmail.com}
\address[ind]{Independent Researcher}

\begin{abstract}
What a recorded field is worth to a prediction is reported as one number, but
that number is a functional of design choices --- the baseline, the learner,
the encoding, the target, the split, the field's availability at the decision
time, the register's quality, the metric and the operating point --- that are
rarely stated. We define the incremental predictive performance of a field as
a \emph{specification surface} $V_s(f)$ over a declared, enumerable design
space, and give four objects for estimating and reporting one: an exact
functional-ANOVA decomposition of the surface's variance across axes;
simultaneous max-$t$ bands from a moving-block bootstrap that refits the whole
pipeline inside every draw; \emph{resolution regions} --- uniformly
beneficial, conditionally beneficial, sign-changing, unresolved --- with a
scalar robustness index; and \emph{specification regret}, which measures what
one-number reporting gets wrong. We prove that the relative reduction is not
identified by the data and the baselines alone, and that the metrics invariant
to a monotone recalibration are exactly the rank-based
ones. On \nPairs\ log--target pairs from \nLogs\
public event logs, varying the baseline alone moves the increment by
\spreadMedian\ AUC at the median pair; no axis dominates the disagreement,
whose largest median first-order index is \sobolMaxPct\ while interactions
carry \sobolInteractionPct; and a one-number report misstates the increment's
\emph{sign} for a median \misreportMedianPct\ of the other admissible
specifications. The case study is a configuration management database on
\nCohort\ bank incidents; its answer turns on which of two decision times the
analyst occupies, and we report both surfaces.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A claim that a recorded field is worth something is a comparison. Its second
arm --- performance \emph{without} the field --- is a choice the analyst
makes, and so are the learner that makes the comparison, the encoding it uses,
the outcome it predicts, the way the data are split in time, whether the field
is available at the moment the decision is taken, how completely the register
behind the field is populated, the metric the difference is read in, and the
operating point at which that metric is evaluated. Each of these is routinely
left implicit. The result is reported as one number.

This paper's position is not that such a number depends on those choices ---
that is known, and Section~\ref{sec:related} says by whom. It is that the
dependence is large enough, on real decisions, to change the sign of the
answer; that the right object to report is therefore a surface over a
\emph{declared} design space rather than a point; and that a surface admits
estimation and inference of its own, which is what a methodological
contribution to this journal has to supply.

\paragraph{The estimand.}
Write a specification $s$ for a complete assignment to every design axis, and
\begin{equation}
V_s(f) \;=\; m_\theta\!\left(A_s(D_{\mathrm{train}}, B \cup \{f_q\}),\,
D_{\mathrm{test}}\right)
\;-\;
m_\theta\!\left(A_s(D_{\mathrm{train}}, B),\, D_{\mathrm{test}}\right),
\label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
where $A_s$ is the fitting procedure (learner, encoding, hyperparameters,
calibration), $B$ the baseline feature set, $f_q$ the field under a register
quality mechanism $q$, $m$ the metric and $\theta$ the operating point, and
where $D_{\mathrm{train}}, D_{\mathrm{test}}$ are fixed by the split rule and
the cohort. The \emph{specification surface} is the map
$s \mapsto V_s(f)$ over a design space $\mathcal{S}$ that the analyst declares
and the reader can enumerate.

We say \emph{incremental predictive performance} and not \emph{value}. The
quantity in~\eqref{eq:estimand} measures how much better a model predicts. It
does not measure what the field costs to acquire or maintain, whether acting
on the prediction changes the outcome, or what an organisation would pay for
either. Section~\ref{sec:dca} converts the increment into units a service desk
operates in --- net benefit at a declared exchange rate --- which is as close
to economic value as this evidence can honestly get, and Section~\ref{sec:lim}
says what still separates the two.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A specification surface, formally.} Section~\ref{sec:surface}
defines $\mathcal{S}$, $V_s$, and the relative reduction $R$; states the
conditions under which $R$ is a quantity at all; and proves two results. $R$
is \emph{not identified} by the data, the feature and the baselines: there is
no function carrying the reduction under one metric to the reduction under
another (Proposition~\ref{prop:nonid}). And $R$ is invariant to a strictly
monotone recalibration of the scoring system, for every configuration, exactly
when the metric is rank-based (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}). A third claim,
that no design axis determines another, is a finite computational search and
is reported as one (Result~\ref{res:nonredundancy}), not as a theorem.

\item \textbf{Estimation and inference for a surface.}
Section~\ref{sec:inference} supplies four instruments. (i) An exact
functional-ANOVA decomposition of $\operatorname{Var}_s[V_s(f)]$ into
first-order and total sensitivity indices per axis, so ``which choice matters''
becomes a measurement. (ii) A moving-block bootstrap that resamples the
training half, \emph{refits the entire pipeline}, and evaluates on a resampled
test half, so the interval contains training-sample, model-selection, encoder
and calibrator variability, and the blocks carry the temporal dependence
between cases. (iii) Simultaneous max-$t$ bands over declared families of
cells, so a statement about a whole decision curve has family-wise coverage.
(iv) Fieller intervals for $R$, which report an unbounded or exclusive
confidence set when the denominator is weak instead of a bounded interval that
is not one.

\item \textbf{Resolution regions and a benchmark against one number.} A cell
is \emph{beneficial}, \emph{harmful} or \emph{unresolved} under the
simultaneous band; a surface is uniformly beneficial, conditionally
beneficial, sign-changing or unresolved; and the robustness index
$\rho \in [-1,1]$ summarises it. $\rho = 1$ is the only state in which one
positive number is a safe summary. Against that, \emph{specification regret}
measures what one-number reporting costs: the share of admissible
specifications whose sign disagrees with the reported cell, and the
performance forgone or destroyed by acting on it. Because ``the surface report
declines to speak where the region is not uniform'' is a definitional win and
not a measured one, Section~\ref{sec:rules} also compares three explicit
decision rules under one loss --- and finds that the conservative rule is
\emph{worse} than one number, while a simple majority over the surface is
better by about a fifth.

\item \textbf{A registered multi-log benchmark, and a case study with two
decision times.} The surface is computed through one frozen pipeline on
\emph{every} log--target pair a pre-registered protocol admits --- \nPairs\ of
them, not only the ones where the answer resolves --- with calibration,
unseen-category rates and rolling-origin validation reported for each. The
configuration management database that motivates the study is one case among
them, and its own answer turns on a question the data cannot settle: whether a
knowledge-article reference was written before the incident existed.
Section~\ref{sec:cmdb} defines two decision times, estimates a surface for
each, and traces the increment as a function of the share of references
assumed to be post-hoc, so a reader can place their own belief on the curve.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this paper is not.}
It is not an enterprise procurement conclusion about configuration management
databases. It is not a systematic review of reporting practice; a pilot
measurement of practice is reported in Section~\ref{sec:practice} with its
sampling error, its coder error and its resolution stated, and no claim in the
paper rests on it. And it reports no deployed system: this is a retrospective
study on public benchmarks, offered as an evaluation methodology.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Variable importance and incremental value.}
That a feature's apparent importance depends on what else is in the model is
the standard motivation for conditional and permutation-based importance
\citep{strobl2008conditional,fisher2019models} and for Shapley-value
attributions \citep{lundberg2017unified,covert2020sage}, and it motivates
nonparametric formulations of importance as a population quantity
\citep{williamson2021vimp}, which
average over subsets precisely because a single conditioning set is arbitrary.
The clinical-prediction literature has made the same point about the
\emph{incremental} value of a marker for two decades: the increase in the area
under the ROC curve from adding a predictor depends on how good the existing
model is \citep{pencina2008evaluating,cook2007roc}, is a poor guide to
clinical usefulness \citep{cook2007roc,vickers2008decision}, and is routinely
misread \citep{kerr2011evaluating}. What that literature establishes, and what
we take as given, is that the baseline and the metric are choices. What it
does not supply is an object that reports the whole dependence, an estimator
for it, or inference over it; the recommendation is usually to add a second or
third instrument, not to characterise the set.

\paragraph{Decision-analytic evaluation.}
Decision curve analysis \citep{vickers2006dca,vickers2016net} makes the
operating point explicit by reading net benefit across an exchange rate, and
is the reason the operating point is an axis here rather than a footnote.
Recent work supplies inference for decision curves
\citep{vickers2019simple}; we use simultaneous bands rather than
pointwise ones because a statement about a \emph{band} of thresholds is a
family of statements.

\paragraph{Specification sensitivity.}
That analytic conclusions move with defensible analytic choices is the subject
of multiverse analysis \citep{steegen2016increasing}, specification-curve
analysis \citep{simonsohn2020specification}, vibration of effects
\citep{patel2015assessment} and, in machine learning evaluation, of work on
underspecification \citep{damour2022underspecification} and on the fragility
of benchmark rankings \citep{bouthillier2021variance}. Our design space and
our figures are recognisably in that tradition, and we say so. What this paper
adds to it is specific: a decomposition of the surface's variance into axis
contributions, simultaneous inference over the surface rather than a
distribution of point estimates, region labels with an explicit decision rule,
and a regret benchmark against the conventional report. Specification-curve
analysis, as usually practised, displays the curve and applies a
permutation test to its median; it does not tell a reader which sub-region of
the design space supports a sign, which is the question a buyer of a data
programme actually asks.

\paragraph{Data valuation.}
Shapley-based data valuation \citep{ghorbani2019data,jia2019towards} values
\emph{instances} against a fixed learner and metric; the axes we vary are
exactly the ones held fixed there. The two are complementary: a data value is
itself a point on a specification surface.

\paragraph{Which axis a study varies.}
Some studies do vary one axis deliberately: the metric axis in effort-aware
defect prediction \citep{carka2022effortaware}, the encoding axis in remaining-time
prediction \citep{liu2025encoding}, the inter-case feature block in predictive
process monitoring \citep{senderovich2017intercase}. What is missing is not the
idea of varying an axis but an object that holds several axes at once and says
what their joint variation implies for a sign.

\paragraph{Prediction on incident event logs.}
The empirical setting is predictive process monitoring, surveyed and
benchmarked across real logs
\citep{teinemaa2019outcome,verenich2019survey} on data of the kind process
mining formalises \citep{vanderaalst2016process}. We predict at case creation
from creation-time attributes with no prefix, which is tabular classification
on a process log rather than prefix-based monitoring; the benchmark protocols
in that literature fix a learner, an encoding and a metric per experiment, and
the sensitivity of their conclusions to those fixings is what
Section~\ref{sec:multilog} measures. Configuration management databases and
their data quality are treated in the IT service management literature as an
operational problem \citep{marrone2011itil}; we are not aware of an evaluation
of one against a named prediction task with the design space held open.

\paragraph{Data quality as an axis.}
The effect of data quality on model performance has been studied directly
\citep{mohammed2025dataquality,liu2025encoding}, and in process mining the effect of
low-quality labels and of encoding choices likewise
\citep{comuzzi2025labels}. Those studies vary quality with the feature set
fixed; we vary quality \emph{as one axis of the same surface} on which the
feature set is another, which is what makes the interaction between them
visible.
