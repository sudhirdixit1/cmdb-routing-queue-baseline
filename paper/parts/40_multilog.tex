
\section{Multi-log results}
\label{sec:multilog}

Every number in this section comes from one master surface,
\texttt{results/s01\_surface.csv}, computed by one pass of one pipeline over
\nPairs\ log--target pairs, \nLearners\ learners, \nSplits\ splits, \nQuality\
register-quality conditions, the nested baseline ladder and \nMetrics\
instruments including the \nGrid-point decision-curve grid. The surface has
\nSurfaceRows\ cells. Uncertainty comes from
\texttt{results/s02\_cells.csv} and \texttt{s02\_bands.csv}, which are
\nDrawRows\ nested bootstrap draws.

\subsection{The master table}

\input{tables/master}

Table~\ref{tab:master} is the single frozen table the referee asked for: one
row per log--target pair, the increment at the reference specification with
its simultaneous band, the region label, the robustness index, and the
misreport rate of a conventional one-number report. Every claim in the
abstract, the introduction and the conclusion is read off it. The reference
specification is declared and is the same everywhere: one-hot logistic
regression, the single temporal holdout, the clean register, the intake block
plus the free field as the baseline, ROC AUC.

\subsection{What the choices are worth}

\input{tables/sobol}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figS2_sobol.png}
\caption{First-order sensitivity indices on the headroom scale, per
log--target pair. The bars do not sum to one: the remainder is interaction,
and Table~\ref{tab:sobol} gives the total indices from which it is read.}
\label{fig:sobol}
\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:sobol} gives the first-order sensitivity indices on the
headroom scale, so the five scalar instruments share an axis. The result is
not the one the previous version of this work implied.

\textbf{No single axis dominates.} Across the corpus the median first-order
index is \sobolMetricPct\ for the metric, \sobolSplitPct\ for the split,
\sobolQualityPct\ for the register-quality condition, \sobolLearnerPct\ for
the learner and \sobolRungPct\ for the baseline rung. The largest of those
five --- \sobolMaxAxis, at \sobolMaxPct\ --- explains about a tenth of the
disagreement between analysts. What explains more than any of them is the part
that belongs to no single choice: the median interaction share
$S_{T_i} - S_i$ is \sobolInteractionPct.

\textbf{Which axis dominates is a property of the pair, not of the problem.}
The largest single index anywhere on the corpus is \sobolTopPct, carried by
\sobolTopAxis\ on \sobolTopPair. On the case-study pairs it is the baseline
rung and the learner that carry the variance; on the small permitting logs it
is the split; on the logs with the weakest signal it is the metric. An analyst
who wants to know which of their own choices their answer turns on cannot read
it off somebody else's paper, and that is the argument for computing the
decomposition rather than quoting one.

\textbf{The baseline axis still moves the answer by more than the answer.}
Holding the learner, the split, the register and the metric at the reference
and varying only the baseline rung, the increment moves by \spreadMedian\ AUC
at the median pair and \spreadMax\ at the widest --- larger, on most pairs,
than the increment itself at the reference cell (Table~\ref{tab:master}). The
index and the spread answer different questions. The index says what share of
the \emph{variance} an axis explains while every other axis is also varying;
the spread says how far that one axis moves the answer on its own. Reporting
only the first understates the baseline; reporting only the second, as the
previous version did, overstates it, and we now report both.

Of the \nPairsBanded\ pairs for which simultaneous bands were computed,
\nUniformlyBeneficial\ are uniformly beneficial, \nCondBeneficial\ are
conditionally beneficial, \nSignChanging\ are sign-changing and
\nUnresolvedPairs\ are unresolved; the median robustness index is
\rhoMedian. On a uniformly beneficial surface a single positive number is a
defensible summary and this paper's apparatus is unnecessary. That state is
reached on \nUniformlyBeneficial\ of \nPairsBanded\ pairs.

\subsection{What one number costs}
\label{sec:results-regret}

\input{tables/regret}

This is the benchmark the referee of the previous version asked for, and it is
the result we would most want a reader to take away.

A conventional report --- one learner, the clean register, the single holdout,
ROC AUC --- misstates the \emph{sign} of the increment for a median
\misreportMedianPct\ of the other admissible specifications. The rate is above
$10\%$ on \nMisreportOverTen\ of the \nPairs\ pairs and above $25\%$ on
\nMisreportOverQuarter; it reaches \misreportMaxPct\ on the worst pair and
falls to \misreportMinPct\ on the best. On \nSignVaries\ of \nPairs\ pairs
between a tenth and nine tenths of the admissible cells are positive, so on
almost every pair in this corpus the sign of the increment is a property of
the specification and not of the data.

Table~\ref{tab:regret} attributes the sign disagreement to axes. Holding
everything else fixed and changing one axis, the sign of the increment flips
for a median \flipSplitPct\ of same-everything-else pairs of cells under the
split, \flipLearnerPct\ under the learner, \flipRungPct\ under the baseline
rung, \flipQualityPct\ under the register-quality condition and
\flipMetricPct\ under the metric. The two axes an evaluation paper is most
likely to hold fixed without comment --- which split, which learner --- are
the two that flip the sign most often.

\subsection{Three decision rules under one loss}
\label{sec:rules}

\input{tables/rules}

Saying that the surface report never asserts a sign where the region is not
uniform is a definitional win, and a reader is right to discount it. The
measured version is Table~\ref{tab:rules}. A reader must decide whether to
adopt the field; their own specification is drawn from the admissible set; and
their loss is the performance the field destroys if they adopt and it is
harmful for them, or the performance it would have added if they decline and
it is beneficial for them. Three rules are compared under that loss:
\emph{one-number} (adopt iff the conventional cell is positive),
\emph{uniform} (adopt iff every admissible cell is positive) and
\emph{majority} (adopt iff more than half of them are).

The result is not the one the surface's advocate would have chosen.

\textbf{The conservative surface rule is worse than one number.} Adopting only
where the surface is uniformly beneficial costs a mean regret of
\regretUniformRule\ against the one-number rule's \regretOneNumber: it
declines so often that the performance it forgoes exceeds the performance the
one-number rule destroys. Refusing to speak has a price, and this is it.

\textbf{The majority rule beats one number, by about a fifth.} Adopting where
more than half of the admissible cells are positive costs \regretMajority, a
\regretMajorityGainPct\ reduction against the one-number rule. Under a draw
concentrated near the conventional specification --- a reader who mostly
follows the usual recipe --- the numbers are \regretOneNumberAdj\ and
\regretMajorityAdj, a \regretMajorityGainAdjPct\ reduction. The gain is
smaller under that weighting, which is what one should expect and is why both
are reported.

So the case for the surface is not that it is safer to say nothing. It is that
a decision rule which \emph{uses} the surface --- even the crudest one, a vote
--- is measurably better than a decision rule which uses one cell of it, and
that the region label tells a reader which of the two situations they are in.
On the \nUniformlyBeneficial\ uniformly beneficial pairs every rule agrees; on
the \nSignChanging\ sign-changing pairs they do not, and that is where the
difference above comes from.

\paragraph{What the comparison assumes.} That the reader's own specification
is drawn from the declared admissible set, which is why two weightings are
reported rather than one, and that every cell in that set is equally
defensible, which is why the intercept-only rung is excluded. Neither
assumption is innocuous and both are the analyst's to revisit; the code takes
a weighting as an argument.

\subsection{Calibration, unseen categories, and rolling origin}

\input{tables/calibration}

Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports, for each pair at the reference cell, the
calibration intercept and slope, the Brier score, and the share of test cases
carrying at least one level unseen in training. The unseen-category rate is
the mechanism behind much of the register's behaviour: a register whose
identities do not recur across the split cannot help a model that has never
seen them, and the rate ranges from \unseenMin\ to \unseenMax\ across the
corpus. Calibration is reported because discrimination alone is not a sufficient
description of a model \citep{vancalster2019calibration}. Calibration
slopes below one indicate the overfitting that
high-cardinality one-hot encodings produce, and are the reason a calibrated
learner is one of the four.

Rolling-origin results are in \texttt{results/s01\_surface.csv} under
\texttt{split = rolling$k$} and enter the decomposition as the split axis;
Appendix~\ref{app:rolling} prints them per fold. The single holdout is not the
best estimate of the increment, and reporting it alone was a limitation of the
earlier version of this work.

Table~\ref{tab:sim} is the simulation of Section~\ref{sec:sim}. Reading the
$V_{\mathrm{limit}}$ rows --- the estimand a resampling interval can cover ---
the fixed-model interval attains between \coverageNaiveMin\ and nominal
coverage, with median \coverageNaiveMedian, and the nested interval between
\coverageNestedMin\ and nominal, with median \coverageNestedMedian. Against
$V_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ both intervals undercover in the misspecified worlds by
construction, because the estimator is not consistent for the oracle quantity
there; the largest such gap is \biasOracleMax\ in AUC units. We report it
rather than describing it as boundary behaviour: under misspecification an
interval around the estimator's own limit is not an interval around the
quantity a reader cares about, and no amount of resampling repairs that.
