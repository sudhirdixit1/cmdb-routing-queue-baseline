## 1. What this is, in one paragraph

An evaluation-methodology paper for Elsevier's *Information Systems*: what a
recorded field is worth to a prediction is not one number but a **surface**
over a declared design space, and the paper supplies four objects for
reporting one (an exact functional-ANOVA decomposition, simultaneous max-$t$
bands from a pipeline-refitting bootstrap, *resolution regions* with a
robustness index, and *specification regret*), evidence on 19 log–target pairs
from 13 public event logs, and a case study on a bank's configuration
management database. Two documents are built: the **article**
(`paper/specification_surfaces.pdf`, 64 pp) and its **supplement**
(`paper/supplement.pdf`, 46 pp).

**The governing discipline:** every number in both documents is a LaTeX macro
in `paper/numbers.tex`, generated by `scripts/make_numbers.py` from
`results/*.csv`. **Never type a number into the prose.** A linter fails the
build if you do.

## 6. What is missing — the work, in priority order

### A. Validate the band's family-wise coverage *(the big one)*
Every referee wants this; the methods referee called it "the single most
important thing to fix". **Nothing in the repo measures it** — `s31_simboost.py`
validates the **pointwise** interval only.

The problem: `s21_bands.py` writes two estimates of the same max-$t$ critical
value — the Gaussian multiplier `q` (which the bands use) and the empirical
quantile `q_emp`. `s40_qcheck.py` shows `q` sits **below the entire
order-statistic interval of `q_emp` on 15 of 19 surface families and all 19
decision-curve families**, anti-conservatively, because the draws are
heavy-tailed and a Gaussian multiplier only makes Gaussian excursions.
Resolved cells: 1,150 vs 995 (surface), 3,066 vs **613** (decision curve).

**Do:** a new script (suggest `s41_bandcoverage.py`) that measures family-wise
coverage of the max-$t$ band against a known answer, over synthetic families
whose **size (120–2,966 cells), draw count (33–150) and tail behaviour** match
this corpus, under Gaussian *and* heavy-tailed draws. `s31_simboost.py`
already enumerates population AUC in closed form — reuse that machinery. Then
report it in §4.3/§10 and, if the multiplier fails, switch the reported bands
to the conservative estimator and recompute everything downstream.

### B. Widen the coverage calibration plane in *n*
`s33_calibrate.py` fits the inflation factor at training sizes **2,000 / 4,000
/ 8,000**. The corpus spans **735 to 176,213** — only **3 of 19** pairs are
inside. And §10.3 proves in the same document that $K/n$ alone does not
determine coverage. Every region label, every $\rho$ and the 7.5% headline
inherit this. Currently disclosed in §6.4 and §11, not fixed.
**Do:** add training sizes at both ends (≈750 and ≈50,000+), refit, recompute.

### C. Length
64 + 46 pages. Referees have asked for **32–40** three times, and it went *up*
last round because corrections are disclosure. Concrete cuts they named:
halve §9.1–9.2; compress §4.4's p-versus-interval passage to a paragraph;
fold Remarks 1–3 and Corollary 1 into §3.4's text; halve §6.8; consolidate the
relative-reduction material now spread across §3.3, §4.5 and §7.6. They also
asked for **one summary table, early**, listing the four reporting objects —
what each is for, where defined, which table reports it, what it costs.

## 7. Rules of the road

1. **Never type a number into prose.** Add a macro in `round21_numbers.py`
   (or `round20_numbers.py`), regenerate, and use it.
2. **Every interval endpoint must come from the run the manuscript names.**
   `INTERVAL_SOURCES` in `round21_verify.py` enforces it — a contrast was once
   printed with two different intervals from two runs.
3. **`ESTIMANDS` in `round21_verify.py`** declares which macros may hold a
   quantity a reader would name in one phrase. Two members that disagree is a
   build failure. Add an entry whenever you create a second macro for one idea.
4. **`to_latex(escape=True)` escapes what you pre-escape.** Write `50%`, not
   `50\%`; never put math in a column heading.
5. **A `p{}` column narrower than its longest unbreakable token does not wrap
   — it spills**, and `\resizebox` cannot see it because the cell still
   measures its declared width. Check `textcols` widths against content.
6. **`build_journal.py` reports both documents' overfull counts.** Both must
   be zero. It used to report only the article's, and 70 boxes in the
   supplement went unnoticed for four rounds.
7. **A macro is only as safe as its definition.** The false coverage sentence
   came from a macro that was an extremum over a *selected subset* and matched
   its source exactly, so the verifier passed. When you add a macro, write
   down what set it ranges over.
8. Bash heredocs here mangle backslashes — use the Write/Edit tools for
   anything containing LaTeX, and beware `\r`/`\n` inside Python strings.
