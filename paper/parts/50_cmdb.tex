\section{Case study: a configuration management database}
\label{sec:cmdb}

A configuration management database is the register an IT estate keeps of the
items it contains, and it is bought partly on the argument that operational
analytics need it. This section evaluates one against a named decision on a
public log: will this case be reassigned to another group before it is
resolved? --- a binary forecast issued at a declared origin. The estate is a bank's and the log is BPI Challenge 2014
\citep{vandongen2014bpic}; the register field is the affected configuration
item, which takes \cardTauTwo\ distinct values on this estate's cohort at
incident creation. It is reported at greater length than any of the \nPairs\
log--target pairs of Section~\ref{sec:multilog} because it is the case that
motivated the study and the one on which the forecast origin --- when in the
service-desk process the forecast is issued --- decides the answer.

\subsection{One log, two answers, and what separates them}
\label{sec:cohort}


This log appears twice in this paper. Section~\ref{sec:multilog} applies the
registered generic rules of Section~\ref{sec:design}, which admit
\nRegisteredCohort\ cases and define the target by the registered handover
rule. This section uses the cohort and the reassignment target defined for the
estate: \nCohort\ cases at prevalence \casePrevalence\ (\casePrevalenceTest\
in the test half), \nCaseTrain\ in
training and \nCaseTest\ in test. The estate's cohort is a strict subset of
the registered one: the \nExtraCases\ cases the registered rules admit on top
of it are those the estate's own cutoff excludes.

The two produce different answers about the register at the later decision
time, and the difference is large enough to change the sign. Against intake
plus group plus the knowledge reference, the estate's cohort and reassignment
target give \VTtwoKnow\ and the registered cohort and handover target give
\VregisteredTauTwo, on which the planned contrast \textsf{PC3}'s one-sided
$p$-value rejects at the Holm-adjusted level while its interval does not
exclude zero (Section~\ref{sec:planned}). Table~\ref{tab:cohort} takes the
manuscript from the first to the second one factor at a time.

\textbf{It is the target, not the cohort.} Over the cohort $\times$ target
design the target carries \shareTargetPct\ of the variance in the increment,
the cohort \shareCohortPct, and their interaction \shareCohortTargetPct.
Reassignment to another group before resolution has prevalence
\casePrevalence\ over the estate's cohort; handover between activity groups,
under the registered rule, has prevalence \prevRegisteredHandover\ over the
registered cohort. They are not the same question and the
register is not worth the same to them. Two further candidate explanations
were checked and neither is one: the two analyses reach the same attribute
values on every row, so it is not a data-preparation difference; and they run
the same pipeline at the same reference cell, so it is not a modelling
difference.

\textbf{An axis nobody declares.} Both analyses sort the log in time and split at
seventy per cent of the row order, and \nTiedTimestamps\ incidents share
their timestamp with another; the sort an earlier, unpublished analysis of
this log used was not stable, so its tie order was neither declared nor
reproducible, and it printed \VpublishedTauTwo\ where the two declared rules
both give \VTtwoKnow\ (recomputed in the archive from that analysis's own
loader, \cohortloader). Over \nTiebreakDraws\ random tie-breaks the
increment ranges over \tiebreakRange\ AUC without changing sign, so no
conclusion turns on it --- but that range is \tiebreakRangeSharePct\ of the
increment's magnitude, produced by a choice no reporting standard asks anyone
to state, which is why Supplement~\ref{app:standard} now asks. Every number
in this section is computed under a declared tie-break, by incident
identifier, on the estate's cohort and reassignment target; the registered
cohort's answers are among the \nPairs\ pairs of Section~\ref{sec:multilog},
so the decision-time result below is a statement about a routing decision on
this estate and not about handover in general.

\subsection{Three forecast origins}
\label{sec:twodecision}

A forecast origin fixes three things at once --- which cases are in scope, what
is known about them, and which fields are admissible --- and the estate's
surface is estimated at each of three moments.

\textbf{The register's worth turns on which one the analyst occupies}: at
$\tau_0$ it is \VtauZero\ [\VtauZeroLo, \VtauZeroHi] over \nTauZero\
\emph{interactions} --- calls to the desk, of which only a minority ever

from zero. The step from $\tau_1$ to $\tau_2$ is an information step
only on the matched population: \nTauTwoUnmatched\ of the \nTauTwo\
incidents have no interaction record, so the \tauMatchedRow\ row of
Table~\ref{tab:tau} restricts $\tau_2$ to $\tau_1$'s own cases and it is that
row, not the \tauPlainRow, that isolates the information.
Read as steps, the ladder moves by \deltaCohortAtTau\ AUC from $\tau_0$ to
$\tau_1$, which is the population; by \deltaSnapshotAtTau\ from $\tau_1$ to
$\tau_2$ on the matched population, which is the information; and by
\deltaPopulationResidual\ more from the matched population to the full one,
which is the incidents without an interaction record.

\subsection{The leakage tipping point, and register quality}
\label{sec:quality}

Two further experiments on this estate are in
Supplement~\ref{app:casedetail}. The first is a leakage tipping point: at
$\tau_2$ the knowledge reference takes the register's increment to
\VTtwoKnow\ [\VTtwoKnowLo, \VTtwoKnowHi], indistinguishable from zero, and
writing $\lambda$ for the share of that field's values assumed written
\emph{after} the incident, the increment reaches half its knowledge-free
value at $\lambda = \lambdaHalf$. An unprovable admissibility assumption is
therefore reported as a sensitivity analysis and not as a headline. The
second is a
register-quality axis under the \nQualityKinds\ declared mechanisms of
Supplement~\ref{app:measurechoice} and, beside them, three dependent mechanisms and a
discovery lag (Supplement~\ref{app:casedetail}), applied to both halves of
the data with their parameters estimated from training alone
(Supplement~\ref{app:degrade}).


\subsection{What this says to a configuration management programme}
\label{sec:programme}

Supplement~\ref{app:programmeread} gives the reading a service-management
audience will want, stated in the estate's own vocabulary: the three
forecast origins in service-desk terms, which layer of the register pays, and
what would have to change for the answer to move. It is a reading of
\emph{one} estate on \emph{one} public log and it is offered as one;

against \deliveredPerThousand\ delivered.

\subsection{The absorption, and why the ratio is secondary}
\label{sec:absorption}

The quantity a report of this kind usually leads with is the \emph{relative}
reduction $R$: the share of the register's apparent value that a single free
field absorbs. The absolute absorption $D = V(f \mid B_0) - V(f \mid B_1)$ is
reported first, with its own interval, and $R$ second with a Fieller set and
the set's kind; on this estate at the reference cell $D$ is \Dabs\ [\DabsLo,
\DabsHi] and $R$ is \Rref\ with a \RrefKind\ Fieller set. The full ladder
and the reason the ratio is secondary are in Supplement~\ref{app:absorption}.
