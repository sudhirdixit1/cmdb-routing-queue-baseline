
\section{Decision-analytic evaluation}
\label{sec:dca}

An increment in AUC is not a quantity a service desk can act on. This section
reads the same surface in net benefit --- the decision-based value of a
forecast in the sense of \citet{murphy1993good} and
\citet{granger2000economic} --- which is the number of correctly nominated
cases per thousand arrivals, net of the false nominations, at a declared
exchange rate between the two.

\subsection{The operating point is a cost ratio, and calibration comes first}
\label{sec:calibfirst}

At threshold $\theta$ the net benefit of a rule that nominates when
$\hat p \geq \theta$ is
\[
\mathrm{NB}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{TP}(\theta)}{n}
\;-\; \frac{\mathrm{FP}(\theta)}{n}\cdot\frac{\theta}{1-\theta},
\]
so $(1-\theta)/\theta$ is the number of false nominations the desk accepts
for one true one, and choosing $\theta$ is choosing an operational cost ratio
that belongs to the analyst's declaration rather than to a grid search. We
report the whole grid --- \nGrid\ points from \gridLo\ to \gridHi\ --- and
name three points a desk might occupy: $\theta = \thetaCheap$ (\ratioCheap\
false nominations per true one, a cheap review), $\thetaBase$ (\ratioBase, a
review and a miss costing about the same) and $\thetaDear$ (\ratioDear,
scarce review capacity); a reader whose desk sits elsewhere reads their own
point off Figure~\ref{fig:dca}. A threshold is a statement about
probability, so every decision curve here is computed on \emph{calibrated}
scores, with the calibrator fitted inside the training half and rebuilt
inside every bootstrap refit, and a pre-specified rule excludes an arm whose
calibration slope leaves \calSlopeWindow\ rather than repairing it, because
recalibration is already a level of the pipeline axis
(Supplement~\ref{app:dcadetail}).

\subsection{Simultaneous bands change what can be said}
\label{sec:dcabands}

The decision-curve family is declared separately from the scalar surface and
carries its own max\nobreakdash-$t$ critical value, because net benefit and a scalar
metric are not the same scale. Its critical value is the empirical
quantile of Section~\ref{sec:simbands}, taken over the \emph{admissible}
family: a cell whose net-benefit difference is exactly zero in at least nine
draws of ten is zero by arithmetic, not by estimation, and is excluded before
the quantile is taken because it is not a statement about the register while
its vanishing spread inflates the value every other cell is judged by
(Section~\ref{sec:simband}). On the case-study family that rule excludes
\nDcaCellsExcludedDegenerate\ of \nDcaFamily\ cells and gives a critical
value of \dcaQEmp\ [\dcaQEmpLo, \dcaQEmpHi], against \dcaQ\ from the
multiplier approximation. The empirical band is the better of the two but
remains about a percentage point short of nominal on its matched family,
which fits this corpus less closely than the surface families do
(Section~\ref{sec:simband}).

Across the \nDcGrid\ operating points of the case-study curve \textbf{the
pointwise interval declares the increment resolvably positive at
\nBeneficialPointwise\ of them and the simultaneous band at
\nBeneficialSimultaneousEmp}, the lowest threshold the second resolves
being $\theta = \thetaBeneficialEmpMin$; the harmful counts are
\nHarmfulPointwise\ against \nHarmfulSimultaneousEmp. All are built from the
same draws, so what differs is the family and the level it is bought at:
those points are the price of a statement about a \emph{range} of thresholds
rather than about each one separately. Supplement~\ref{app:dcabandsfull}
gives the counts under the multiplier, under the order-statistic upper end of
the critical value and with the excluded cells kept, none of which changes
the conclusion; Figure~\ref{fig:dca} draws the curve (Table~\ref{tab:dca} is
a score-threshold sensitivity analysis on raw scores, not this curve). The
pointwise interval is not widened by a family-wise factor, and no $(n,K)$
widening is applied to this family.

\subsection{The decision, in units a service desk uses}
\label{sec:nbregret}

The comparison is run in net benefit per thousand cases, over a declared
distribution of exchange rates a desk plausibly holds
(\deskQlo\ to \deskQhi, centred \deskQmid), and only on models that pass the
pre-specified calibration rule --- which excludes \nModelsDcaExcluded\ of
\nModelsDca\ models, a model being a pair--pipeline--baseline triple whose
two arms must both pass, and \nPairsDcaExcluded\ of \nPairs\ pairs.
This is the most consequential operational finding: a decision curve read off
a model whose probabilities are wrong is read at a threshold the model does
not have.

\textbf{The four decision rules separate on most pairs, with the excess
falling on the conservative rule rather than the one-number rule}
(Supplement~\ref{app:deskfull}). Larger than anything the choice of decision
rule costs a one-number report is which fields the forecast origin makes
admissible: on the case-study log's pre-specified handover target, the
register promises \promisedPerThousand\ per thousand against the arrival
attributes alone and delivers \deliveredPerThousand\ once the assignment
group and the knowledge reference --- assumed recorded by incident creation
--- are in the benchmark, a shortfall of \shortfallPerThousand. Supplement~\ref{app:deskfull} carries the rules, the
exchange-rate distribution and the exclusions; Tables~\ref{tab:desk}
and~\ref{tab:rules} the numbers.
